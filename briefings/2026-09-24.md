# Web3 Threat Briefing — 2026-09-24

> **Scope:** Multi-chain (EVM/Solidity · Solana/Rust · Cosmos/Move · cross-chain bridges). Priority: DeFi, wallets, key infrastructure.
> **Window:** Last ~24–48h (September 22–24 UTC). **Headline: A Cosmos-ecosystem governance takeover on Neutron drained Astroport and Drop for a combined ~$9.4M and forced both Neutron and the Cosmos Hub itself to halt block production — the first time a governance exploit on a consumer chain has forced its parent chain to shut down. A second, independently confirmed incident (FomoPeek, an App Store iOS app hiding a kernel-exploit framework) adds a concrete data point to the mobile-wallet-key-theft threat with a real traced loss figure. The DCENT App Wallet incident's true scope just got a lot bigger on re-tracing.**

## 1. 🔴 Active exploits (last 24h)

### Neutron Governance Takeover — Astroport + Drop, ~$9.4M

**Chain:** Cosmos (Neutron consumer chain; spillover halt on Cosmos Hub)
**Status:** CONFIRMED. Attack executed **Sept 22** (~10:24pm ET / ~Sept 23 02:24 UTC). Neutron halted block production shortly after (Sept 22, 3:43am ET per on-chain height) and its own resumption status is **not confirmed as of this writing** — public reporting through Sept 23 addresses only the Cosmos Hub's restart, not Neutron's. Cosmos Hub, which had also halted to isolate a stolen-ATOM wallet, resumed block production **Sept 23 at 12:00 UTC** after ~24h48m.
**Impact:** ~$9.4M total (GoPlus Security), split as **~$4.9M from Astroport** and **~$4.4M from Drop (DropDotMoney)**. ~$1.96M was bridged off Neutron/Cosmos within hours of the attack. On Cosmos Hub's first post-restart block, validators moved **1,227,121 ATOM (~$2.1–2.2M)** out of the attacker-linked wallet via a coordinated transaction.
**What happened:** The attacker bought 31.62 million NTRN tokens for roughly $20,199 just 11 minutes before the tally closed on a Neutron governance proposal (disguised under a title referencing "AI Agent Takeover" / AI governance research), swinging the vote to ~82% approval. The proposal invoked Neutron's chain-level `MsgUpdateAdmin` capability, which can rewrite a CosmWasm contract's designated admin address — overriding the app-level multisig controls that Astroport and Drop relied on for protection. Within 24 minutes of the proposal executing, the attacker used the seized admin rights to migrate 10 of 11 targeted contracts to malicious code and drain them, then routed proceeds across Neutron, Cosmos Hub, Noble, Axelar, dYdX, Osmosis and Ethereum addresses.
**Root cause:** a structural/economic-design flaw rather than a code bug — Neutron's `wasmd`-based governance grants chain governance the power to override any contract's app-level admin, but the cost of winning a governance vote (~$20K in NTRN, against liquid governance stake worth roughly $113K at the time) was far lower than the value of the contracts that authority controlled ($9.4M). No timelock or minimum-holding-period existed between acquiring voting power and a proposal's tally/execution.
**Bug class:** access-control / governance-privilege-escalation (chain-level admin override of app-level multisig), economic-design (governance-attack cost far below protected-asset value).

### FomoPeek — Malicious App Store iOS "Crypto Tracker" with Kernel Exploit Framework, ~$580K

**Chain:** iOS (mobile client); stolen funds moved across multiple chains
**Status:** CONFIRMED. Joint SlowMist + OKX security team investigation published **Sept 23**. Malicious app versions (1.1, 1.2) were live on Apple's App Store **Sept 9–17**; a clean version 1.3 replaced them Sept 17. Primary attacker address became active **Sept 15**.
**Impact:** ~$580,000 (579,984 USDT) traced to a single attacker-controlled address; funds moved through FixedFloat, KuCoin and cce.cash.
**What happened:** FomoPeek, marketed as a crypto price-tracking app, contained two hidden native modules (internally named `apptrace` and `libapptracecore`) unrelated to its stated functionality. These modules supported remote configuration and carried a kernel-exploit framework with **eight distinct exploit methods**, auto-selected based on device model and installed iOS version (covering iOS 12.0–18.7.2 and 26.0–26.1). Once triggered, the framework could escape Apple's app sandbox, decrypt the device Keychain, and read data from 19 other installed apps — including MetaMask, Trust Wallet, SafePal, OKX Wallet and Apple Notes.
**Root cause:** App Store review failed to catch a native kernel-exploit payload hidden behind a legitimate-looking utility app; once installed, iOS's app-sandbox and Keychain protections were defeated by the exploit chain itself rather than bypassed via a logic flaw in any wallet app.
**Bug class:** client-implementation (OS-level sandbox/Keychain compromise via App-Store-distributed malware, not a wallet-app logic bug).

## 3. 🟢 Advisories & patches

### XRPL Batch amendment — pre-mainnet "signer gap" found and fixed before activation

**Disclosed Sept 22–23.** Security researcher Pranamya Keshkamat, working with Cantina AI's autonomous static-analysis tool Apex, found a critical logic flaw in the not-yet-activated XRPL **Batch** amendment's signer-validation function: the loop would immediately declare validation successful and exit as soon as it encountered a signer whose account did not yet exist on the ledger but whose signing key matched their own account — skipping validation of every remaining signer in the batch. This would have allowed a transaction to appear multi-signed and authorized when it was not. The XRPL team shipped a corrected proposal, **BatchV1_1**, removing the early-exit condition and tightening signer-authorization checks. The amendment is tracking toward activation on **Sept 29, 2026, 14:06 UTC**, contingent on validators holding an 80%+ supermajority for the full 14-day signaling window — not yet guaranteed. Coverage notes that even with the ledger-level fix, **client applications that independently implement or assume the old batch-signing semantics remain at risk** until they update.
**Bug class:** logic-error (early-exit in a multi-signer validation loop, skipping remaining signature checks) — caught pre-mainnet via static analysis, not exploited in production.

## 4. 🎯 What to hunt

**1. Chain-level governance authority overriding app-level admin/multisig controls (Cosmos/CosmWasm)**

Neutron's `MsgUpdateAdmin` let chain governance rewrite any CosmWasm contract's admin address, bypassing the app-level multisig that Astroport and Drop assumed was their real protection boundary. If scope includes any Cosmos-SDK chain or CosmWasm-based protocol, check: (a) can chain governance reassign contract admin rights independent of the app's own access controls? (b) is there a timelock or minimum bonding period between a proposal's tally and its execution — long enough to react to a suspicious last-minute vote swing? (c) what's the ratio between the cost of winning a governance vote (tokens needed, at spot price, right before tally) and the value of assets that vote's outcome can control? Neutron's ratio was roughly $20K to seize control of $9.4M — a number worth calculating for every governance-gated protocol in scope.

**2. Just-in-time voting-power acquisition to swing quorum**

Both this Neutron attack and the earlier BonkDAO precedent share a pattern: buy governance tokens in the final minutes before a vote closes, using spot balance rather than a snapshot, to swing an otherwise low-turnout vote. Check whether voting power is calculated from a pre-proposal snapshot (resistant to this) or from balance-at-tally (vulnerable), and whether proposal descriptions are checked for disguised/misleading intent before automated execution.

**3. iOS App Store supply-chain — native modules hidden inside legitimate-looking utility apps**

FomoPeek's kernel-exploit framework was distributed as ordinary app updates and stayed live for over a week before detection. For wallet-adjacent mobile app audits: check whether an app requests entitlements or dynamic-code-loading capability disproportionate to its stated function, whether Keychain items are scoped as narrowly as possible, and whether the wallet app itself can detect a compromised host environment (jailbreak/exploit indicators, unusual cross-app data access) rather than trusting a benign OS.

**4. Client-side signing implementations in hardware/software wallets — blast radius keeps growing**

DCENT's XRP-only tracing has now roughly doubled from initial estimates (~$9.3M → ~$18.7M) purely through better on-chain tracing, with the underlying signing-key-exposure mechanism still undisclosed after 10 days. Treat any "preliminary" loss figure attached to a client-side key-compromise incident as a floor, not a ceiling, and prioritize signing/key-derivation code review in any hardware or mobile wallet audit.

**5. Multi-signer validation loops — no early-exit on partial success**

XRPL's Batch signer-gap is a clean, generalizable lesson: any signer-validation loop that exits as soon as it finds one valid signer (rather than requiring every listed signer to independently validate or explicitly fail) can be tricked by placing a trivially-valid signer first. Worth a direct grep in any multisig/batch-transaction code for early-return patterns inside signature-verification loops.

**6. VM/client-layer atomicity and shared-key blast-radius — still open, still unresolved**

MultiversX's VM-level atomicity bug (Day 6, restart still undetermined) and the Fetch.ai/SingularityNET shared bridge-key incident (Day 6) remain live, unresolved bug classes in production — not hypothetical ones. Standing guidance unchanged: enumerate every contract/token/chain trusting a shared authorizer key before scoping a bridge audit as "one converter, one key."
