### Coldcard Bitcoin Entropy Exploit — Day 17: Coinkite Compensation Framework Still Pending

**Chain:** Bitcoin
**Status:** ONGOING — no new confirmed Wave 5 drain in the Aug 14–16 window. Coinkite's promised victim-compensation framework has not been published. Roughly 210,000 BTC has migrated out of long-term cold storage as users re-evaluate legacy Coldcard seeds. FBI / DOJ crypto asset recovery unit involvement confirmed Aug 5.
**Cumulative losses:** ~$116–130M (5,200+ addresses; four documented attack waves since Aug 4, 2026)

**Root cause (unchanged):** A build-configuration error in Coldcard firmware v4.0.1 (March 2021 release) silently substituted a weak software PRNG for the hardware TRNG during seed generation. Effective private-key entropy collapsed from 128 bits to ~40 bits — brute-forceable offline without physical wallet access. At least 15 separate attacker groups have been exploiting the disclosed technique in parallel.

**Remediation matrix (unchanged — if you haven't acted, act now):**

| Device | Affected firmware | Required action |
|---|---|---|
| Coldcard Mk3 | v4.0.0 – v5.0.3 | **Move ALL funds immediately** — no firmware fix exists |
| Coldcard Mk4 / Mk5 | < v5.6.0 | Upgrade to ≥ v5.6.0, generate fresh seed, migrate funds |
| Coldcard Q series | < v1.5.0Q | Upgrade to ≥ v1.5.0Q, generate fresh seed, migrate funds |

> Seeds generated via dice-roll entropy are **not** affected. Any seed created on Mk3 or pre-upgrade Mk4/Mk5/Q while affected firmware was active must be treated as compromised.

**Bug class:** key-management

### Ravencoin KAWPOW Consensus Exploit — Day 9: Convergence Status Still Unknown

**Chain:** Ravencoin (PoW, KAWPOW algorithm)
**Status:** ACTIVE / IN RECOVERY — no public declaration of chain convergence as of Aug 15 UTC. Mining pools 2Miners and RavenMiner (jointly controlling ~85% of the ~62% identified hash rate) continue mining the clean branch from checkpoint block 4,487,775 with emergency patch v4.6.1.1-hf1. Exchanges including Upbit, Bitget, and Bitvavo remain suspended for RVN deposits/withdrawals.

**Root cause:** The `nHeight` field in KAWPOW block header validation was supposed to be checked against a block's actual chain position but was never enforced. Attackers exploited this to produce invalid blocks orders of magnitude more cheaply than legitimate mining, first demonstrated at block 4,487,776 on Aug 7, 2026.

**Outstanding risk:** If the clean chain overtakes the attacker's branch, a reorg of ~3 days of transactions will execute. Any RVN received or sent between block 4,487,776 and the reorg height could be rolled back.

**Action:** Do NOT re-enable RVN deposits or treat any RVN confirmation above block 4,487,775 as final until the Ravencoin core team or 2Miners officially declares convergence.

**Bug class:** logic-error (consensus validation)
**Sources:**

### BTCPay Server LND Macaroon Auth-Bypass — Day 9

**Chain:** Lightning Network (Bitcoin)
**Status:** Patch v2.4.2 available since Aug 8. Critical residual: LND admin `.macaroon` credentials **never expire** — any node accessed pre-patch carries a live full-admin credential that upgrading BTCPay alone doesn't revoke.

**Remediation checklist (if not yet complete):**
1. Confirm BTCPay ≥ v2.4.2
2. Stop BTCPay + LND → delete `~/.lnd/data/chain/bitcoin/mainnet/{admin,invoice,readonly}.macaroon` → restart LND → restart BTCPay (LND regenerates fresh macaroons)
3. Audit LND logs for unexpected `SendPayment`, `CloseChannel`, or `ConnectPeer` calls since Aug 1

### OWASP Smart Contract Top 10 2026 — Calibration Update for Hunters

**Released:** February 2026 (correlating with 2025 incident data; increasingly validated by 2026 Q1–Q2 losses of $840M+)
**Why it matters today:** The 2026 rankings substantially diverge from intuition shaped by 2022–2023 incidents. Hunters calibrating their audit scope on older mental models are likely under-weighting the highest-payout classes.

**2026 Top 5 (ranked by incident impact from 2025 data):**

| Rank | Category | 2026 note |
|---|---|---|
| #1 | **Access Control** | Accounts for >50% of 2026 DeFi losses by value — role misconfigurations, unchecked `msg.sender`, admin key exposure |
| #2 | **Business Logic** | Climbed from lower ranks; subtle invariant violations that pass unit tests but fail in adversarial sequences (e.g., flash-loan ordering, multi-tx manipulation) |

**Hunt implication:** For any protocol in your scope, the highest-EV path in 2026 is: (1) map every privileged role and its assignment path, (2) test business-logic invariants under flash-loan-aided state manipulation, (3) verify oracle staleness/deviation checks before any payout calculation.

### Allbridge Core Solana — Repeated Flash-Loan Pool-Ratio Exploit (July 19, Pattern Spotlight)

**Chain:** Solana (attacker bridged proceeds to Ethereum)
**Amount:** ~$1.65M USDC (second Allbridge flash-loan exploit; first was April 2023, $573K)
**Root cause:** Allbridge had committed post-2023 to deploy a single liquidity pool per blockchain to prevent same-transaction pool manipulation. The Solana deployment retained a USDC+USDT dual-token pool architecture — precisely the configuration the fix was supposed to eliminate. Attacker borrowed $1.12M from Kamino on Solana, rapidly swapped USDC↔USDT to skew pool ratios, withdrew at favorable rates, bridged to Ethereum, and routed through privacy pools.

**Why it's here today:** This is the canonical **incomplete-fix** pattern. The protocol had a post-mortem, published a remediation, implemented it — but missed one deployment. In your bounty targets, look for protocols whose changelogs reference a previous exploit: the incomplete deployment of the fix is a near-guaranteed finding class. Check every chain deployment individually against the stated remediation.

**Bug class:** flash-loan, price-manipulation (pool accounting)

### CVE-2026-48054 — OpenZeppelin Contracts Wizard Code Injection (Ongoing — Day 66)

**Affected:** `@openzeppelin/wizard` npm ≤ 0.10.8
**Severity:** HIGH (CVSS 8.8) | **Advisory:** GHSA-4x76-22x2-rx8v

Unsanitized `opts.name` / `opts.uri` are interpolated into generated Hardhat (`test/test.ts`) and Foundry (`test/<Name>.t.sol`) test scaffolds without escaping. A crafted input breaks out of string-literal context and executes arbitrary code when a developer runs `npm test` or `forge test`. CI/CD pipelines that auto-generate Wizard output and immediately test it are especially exposed.

**Fix:** `npm install @openzeppelin/wizard@^0.10.9`
**Hosted wizard.openzeppelin.com is already patched** — local/CI installs that haven't updated remain vulnerable.

## 4. 🎯 Hunt hooks

**1. Hunt incomplete-fix deployments across multi-chain protocols.**
The Allbridge repeat is a template. For any protocol in your scope that had a previous exploit or post-mortem: (a) Read the original post-mortem to understand exactly which contracts/functions were patched. (b) Check every chain deployment individually — pull deployed bytecode and compare against the patched source. (c) Look specifically at Solana, Base, Arbitrum, and Polygon deployments of protocols whose primary fix was applied to their Ethereum mainnet instance. Same-deployment-pattern, different-chain-address mismatches are a near-guaranteed finding class. Many teams patch the chain where the exploit happened and forget the mirror deployments.

**2. Map access-control role hierarchies before anything else (OWASP #1 for 2026).**
For any EVM protocol you're auditing: (a) Build a full role graph — enumerate every `AccessControl` role, `Ownable` owner, `TimelockController` proposer/executor, and `Governor` role from the contract source. (b) Trace how each role can be granted/revoked: is there a path for any role to self-escalate, or for an unrelated contract to grant a role? (c) Check whether admin functions are protected by `onlyOwner`/`onlyRole` *and* whether those modifiers check the right role (copy-paste errors in role name strings are surprisingly common). (d) Verify timelocks: does every privileged setter have an enforced delay? Access-control bugs at #1 in 2026 means they are the most under-reviewed and highest-EV category right now.

**3. Monitor the Ether.fi situation with on-chain tooling — possible first-mover window.**
Three days of silence from a protocol that has previously foiled an attack means one of two things: false alarm (likely), or a breach being managed without disclosure (less likely but non-zero). Either way, there is an on-chain forensics opportunity. The steps: (a) Pull all transactions from Ether.fi-associated contract addresses since Aug 12 on Ethereum and check for large withdrawal queue entries, unusual ETH/staking-token outflows, or calls to `withdrawFromArks`/withdrawal-queue drains. (b) Check whether any EigenLayer delegation or restaking adapter contracts had unexpected token movements. (c) Look at MEV/sandwich transactions around any large withdrawal events — front-running a large vault withdrawal is a known attack pattern. If this resolves as a false alarm, the forensics practice is still valuable for any ERC-4626-style vault you cover.
