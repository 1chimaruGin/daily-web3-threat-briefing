# Web3 Threat Briefing — 2026-10-03

> **Scope:** Multi-chain (EVM/Solidity · Solana/Rust · Cosmos/Move · cross-chain bridges). Priority: DeFi, wallets, key infrastructure.
> **Window:** Last ~24–48h (October 1 – October 3 UTC). No briefing was produced for October 2; this edition covers the full gap. Two new, comparatively small incidents cleared the bar — a cross-chain intents exploit that was fully reimbursed within 24 hours, and an isolated third-party-adapter theft that left Aave's and Safe's core contracts untouched. The larger story this window is incremental: a formal joint Mandiant/SlowMist report on the Bitget breach, blunt recovery-expectation comments from Bitget's CEO, and Zano's long-promised post-mortem.

## 1. 🔴 Active exploits (last 24h)

### NEAR Intents — Omni Deposit/Withdrawal Logic Flaw, ~$3.8M — RESOLVED, funds returned in full

**Chain:** NEAR Intents cross-chain swap infrastructure (operates across 35 chains; the exploited leg involved USDT on BNB Smart Chain). Deposits/withdrawals on 11 networks, including Polygon and Optimism, were paused precautionarily.
**Status:** RESOLVED. Exploit occurred Thursday, Oct 1, 2026. NEAR Intents paused all services immediately and patched the contract-side vulnerability within roughly an hour. The attacker returned the entire ~$3.8M on **Oct 2, 2026 at 14:30 UTC** (confirmed via a BNB Chain transaction), with a message reading "We've returned all the funds, we were in the wrong... Remember to always use bug bounties!" NEAR co-founder Illia Polosukhin said the team identified the responsible party within 24 hours using an internal detection layer ("SHIELD") plus manual tracing, but the team explicitly declined to disclose tracing methods or technical root cause, stating the investigation is now closed ("We dropped the investigation already"). On-chain analyst ZachXBT had separately traced the initially stolen funds through KuCoin and on to Bitcoin before the return.
**What happened / root cause:** A logic bug in the interaction between NEAR Intents' Omni deposit/withdrawal infrastructure and its smart contract let the attacker get the contract to validate artificial deposits/withdrawals without posting equivalent collateral. No lower-level technical detail (which function, which chain adapter) has been published, and NEAR has stated it will not be releasing one now that the case is closed.
**Bug class:** bridge-verification-logic (intent-layer deposit/withdrawal validation bypass, cross-chain accounting).

**Notable context:** This incident landed two days after NEAR Intents publicly stated it had blocked a ~$50M swap attempt linked to the Bitget attacker (suspected North Korea-linked). No source establishes any connection between that episode and this exploit — treat any link as speculative.

### Aave v3 — FlashLoopAdapter / Loop Safe Module Access-Control Exploit, ~114.09 ETH (~$305K)

**Chain:** Ethereum (third-party "Loop Safe Module" adapter used alongside Aave v3 and Safe multisig wallets; not Aave's own core contracts, not Safe's core contracts).
**Status:** UNRESOLVED as of this briefing. Exploit identified by the SlowMist Security Team, with activity dated Oct 1-2, 2026. Only two Safe multisig wallets (the ones with the Loop module enabled) were affected; investigation is reportedly still open as to whether additional wallets/contracts are exposed. Aave's founder publicly clarified that Aave v3's core lending pools were not affected — the vulnerability lived entirely in the third-party adapter's own access-control logic.
**What happened:** The FlashLoopAdapter's `open()`/`close()` functions verified a caller only by checking `ISafe(msg.sender).isModuleEnabled(address(this)) == true`, without independently verifying the caller's actual identity. The attacker deployed a forged Safe contract that always returns `true` for this check, passing verification. The adapter's `_swap()` function then executed a raw call to an attacker-supplied router with attacker-controlled calldata; by pointing the router at a victim Safe and the calldata at `execTransactionFromModule` (a legitimate Safe function available to any enabled module), the attacker turned the adapter's own module permissions into remote control over the victim Safes. The attacker drained weETH and Aave collateral from the two Safes, then used a Morpho flash loan to repay ~1,300 WETH in debt to unlock the collateral before converting to ETH.
**Root cause / bug class:** access-control (trusting a module-enabled check as a proxy for caller identity, combined with an unrestricted arbitrary-call `_swap()` path).

### Bitget — Third-Party Zero-Day → Forged Admin Credentials → Pre-Signature Transaction Forgery, ~$387.5M (Day 10)

**Chain:** Multi-chain exchange infrastructure (Ethereum, BNB Chain, Avalanche, TRON, Zcash)
**Status:** ONGOING recovery (Day 10, from Sept 24 detection). A joint **Mandiant + SlowMist** investigation was published Oct 1, giving the fullest technical account yet, while still **not naming the exploited vendor(s)** — referred to only as "Product A" and "Product B." Per the report: malicious activity traces back to **Aug 31, 2026**, when a zero-day in a service running on "Product A" was exploited; the attacker ran a hidden script under that service process to extract database credentials, then (per Mandiant) deployed a web shell onto "security appliance B" and established a C2 connection to move laterally into Bitget's wallet environment. The actual theft transactions executed **Sept 25 at 1:49 AM** (consistent with the previously reported Sept 24 detection window). On Oct 2, CEO Gracy Chen told CNBC that only **~$1.1M of the $387.5M has been frozen** (by Circle, Tether and NEAR Intents) and that Bitget is **"not expecting to recover a lot"** of the stolen funds, citing the track record of recovery from past exchange hacks. The Protection Fund remains replenished to ~$300M from Bitget's own reserves; user balances were not affected. No change to prior North Korea attribution (Elliptic, TRM Labs) in this window.
**Bug class:** access-control / supply-chain (third-party security-product zero-day → credential theft → lateral movement → forged admin credentials feeding the transaction-approval pipeline).

### Zano — Gateway Address Unauthorized Minting, Chain Rolled Back ~1 Month (Day 7)

**Chain:** Zano (privacy-focused, CryptoNote-based L1)
**Status:** Post-mortem **published Oct 2, 2026**. The attacker registered a Gateway Address and paid a 100 ZANO (~$553) setup fee on **Aug 28**, executed a first mint of ~18.4M ZANO on **Aug 29**, and this went undetected for nearly a month until a second mint of another ~18.4M ZANO plus **1.8 quadrillion fUSD** on **Sept 25** triggered internal detection — total ~36.9M ZANO illegitimately minted. Because the counterfeit coins were functionally indistinguishable from genuine ZANO once minted, they could be freely spent, which is why Zano opted for a full month-long chain rollback rather than a targeted fix. Zano confirmed its AI-assisted testing, internal audits and bug bounty program all failed to catch the flaw pre-exploit. Compensation is to be funded from the developer fund, team personal funds and outside contributions, paid out in stages as exchanges replay reversed withdrawals — no dollar figure or timetable given.
**Bug class:** access-control / asset-issuance-logic (unbounded minting via a privileged account-style balance feature bolted onto a UTXO chain).

## 3. 🟢 Advisories & patches

**XRPL — emergency `xrpld` 3.4.1 release ahead of Oct 9 amendment activation.** Separately from any exploit, the XRP Ledger shipped an emergency security release (xrpld 3.4.1) implementing a new amendment, **fixBatchV1_2**, which rejects Batch inner transactions that use the wrong wrapper type and strengthens integer-arithmetic checks in payment/ledger code — addressing an issue where a Batch inner object whose wrapper is not `RawTransaction` could be processed before amendment-dependent validation runs. This activates alongside the previously tracked **BatchV1_1** amendment, both targeting **Oct 9, ~14:46 UTC**, contingent on validator support holding above 80% (currently 30-of-35, ~85.7%). XRPL has not yet published the underlying source/report for fixBatchV1_2, only the mitigation; no sign of in-the-wild exploitation. Any implementer building on XRPL Batch transactions should track this release — a wrapper-type-confusion bug in a not-yet-live feature is exactly the kind of pre-launch finding worth re-testing independently once the source is published.

## 4. 🎯 What to hunt

**1. Third-party adapters/modules bolted onto audited core protocols (Aave FlashLoopAdapter, new this window)**

The failure pattern here is specific and reusable: a helper contract trusted a Safe-module-enabled check (`isModuleEnabled`) as a stand-in for caller-identity verification, then exposed an arbitrary-call `_swap()` path. Any integration where contract A grants contract B elevated rights (module status, operator role, approval) should be tested for whether B's *other* entry points can be abused by a forged caller that merely satisfies the cheap boolean check B relies on — not by attacking A directly. Core-protocol audits increasingly need to extend to "what can a malicious counterparty do with the module/role we hand out," not just the core logic itself.

**2. Intent/relayer-based bridges — deposit/withdrawal accounting without equivalent collateral checks (NEAR Intents, new this window)**

NEAR Intents' bug let a smart contract validate deposits/withdrawals without actually requiring matching collateral — a classic intents/relayer-layer accounting gap. Any protocol using an off-chain-coordinated "intent" or relayer model to batch cross-chain deposits/withdrawals should be checked for whether the on-chain settlement step independently re-verifies value in, value out — rather than trusting a relayer-reported state. Fast full reimbursement (NEAR, Day 1-2) doesn't change the fact the bug existed in production; treat this as a live class to sweep even though this specific incident is now closed with no root-cause detail published.

**3. Third-party/vendor components inside the transaction-approval path (Bitget, now Day 10)**

Unchanged, reinforced: the Mandiant/SlowMist report confirms the attack chain ran zero-day → stolen DB credentials → web shell on a second security appliance → lateral movement into wallet infra, over nearly a month (Aug 31 to Sept 25) before the theft executed. Map every security/monitoring appliance with write access near custody or signing infrastructure — this class of attack doesn't touch the wallet's own code at all.

**4. Recovery-rate realism vs. "reserve fund" messaging (Bitget, new this window)**

Bitget's CEO now publicly states expectations of minimal fund recovery (~$1.1M frozen of $387.5M) despite the Protection Fund being refilled to $300M. For exchange/custody audits, distinguish "the operator can cover the loss from its own balance sheet" from "the stolen funds will be recovered" — these are different claims with very different risk implications for a second incident.

**5. Post-mortem latency as its own signal, updated**

Zano (Day 7) and Bitget (Day 10, partial) have now actually delivered technical write-ups. Payy, Meter.io, Neutron (partial — recovery mechanics published, root cause already known), DCENT, Fetch.ai, MultiversX and Blink have not, several now 2+ weeks overdue against their own stated targets. Don't wait on official write-ups to reason from on-chain evidence.

**6. Governance-cost-to-TVL ratio in Cosmos-SDK/CosmWasm chains (Neutron, Day 11)**

Unchanged: calculate the ratio between the cost of winning a governance vote at spot price right before tally and the value of assets that vote's outcome can control, and check for timelocks between proposal tally and execution.

**7. Client-side signing implementations in hardware/software wallets (DCENT, Day 19)**

Unchanged: prioritize signing/key-derivation code review in any hardware or mobile wallet audit; DCENT's mechanism is still undisclosed after 19 days.
