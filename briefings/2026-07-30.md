### Crypto DAO — $8.2M | BNB Chain | access-control
**Date:** 2026-07-28–29 UTC

### Garden Finance — $450K | Multi-chain | logic-error / bridge-dvn
**Date:** 2026-07-26–27 UTC
**Chains:** Bitcoin, Ethereum, Arbitrum, Solana (4 blockchains)
**Root cause:** Attacker exploited Garden's HTLC (Hash Time-Lock Contract) implementation. Blockaid raised the initial alarm at 21:30 UTC July 26 describing "ongoing exploit on Garden's HTLC contracts." Garden initially attributed the breach to a solver's backend database, but on-chain analysis pointed to HTLC claim logic — the protocol's atomic-swap solver could claim HTLC outputs on the destination chain without full validation of the source lock, enabling cross-chain fund extraction without completing the swap leg.
**$ lost:** $450K USDT
**Bug class:** logic-error, bridge-dvn
**Source:** [CryptoTimes (Jul 27)](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/07/27/garden-finance-halts-app-after-450k-usdt-exploit-hits-four-blockchains/)

## 2. 🟡 New disclosed findings / techniques

### HTLC Cross-Chain Atomic Swap — Asymmetric Claim Validation
Garden Finance's exploit surfaces a generalizable flaw in HTLC-based bridges and DEX aggregators: if the **destination-chain claim** can be processed by a trusted solver without on-chain proof that the **source-chain lock** is still valid and unspent, an adversary controlling or spoofing the solver role can drain the destination pool without completing the source leg. This is structurally distinct from a bridge DVN compromise — it lives entirely in the application layer of atomic-swap protocols. HTLC implementations on any chain that rely on off-chain solver routing should be reviewed for unilateral claim permissions.
**Applies to:** Any HTLC-based bridge, atomic-swap DEX (THORChain, Maya, Garden Finance forks)
**Source:** [Protos (Jul 23)](https://protos.com/defi-loses-35m-in-a-day-are-bounties-inviting-more-hacks/)

### BNB Chain "Publicly Callable Vault" Pattern — Ongoing Drain Wave
Blockaid's H1 2026 report (released Jul 28) identifies BNB Chain as the largest venue for "long-tail" access-control exploits: anonymous contracts, no audits, vault/treasury functions lacking `onlyOwner` or role-based guards. Seven BNB Chain protocols with this exact pattern were drained between May–July 2026. The Crypto DAO incident is just the latest. Blockaid notes AI-generated Solidity ("vibe-coded" contracts) is now a contributing factor — LLMs frequently omit access modifiers on admin functions unless explicitly prompted.
**Applies to:** Any BNB Chain protocol with unaudited vault/treasury contracts
**Source:** [The Block — Blockaid H1 Report (Jul 28)](https://www.theblock.co/post/409944/crypto-hacks-hit-record-high-in-h1-2026-as-losses-top-1-billion-blockaid-says)

### DPRK / Lazarus July 2026 Pattern — Social Engineering of Validator Key Holders
The AFX Trade ($24.15M, Jul 22) and Wanchain Bridge ($10–13M, Jul 22) incidents both involved compromise of private keys held by bridge validators. Blockaid's H1 analysis confirms Lazarus-linked actors now specifically target **bridge validator operators** via spear-phishing and malicious job-offer documents, bypassing smart-contract logic entirely. 55% of all H1 2026 losses trace back to DPRK-affiliated key-management failures across exchanges, bridges, and staking protocols.
**Applies to:** Any multi-sig bridge with fewer than 10 independent validator operators
**Source:** [Blockaid H1 2026 via CryptoTimes (Jul 29)](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/07/29/crypto-hacks-cross-1-1b-in-record-h1-2026-losses-blockaid/); [TechTimes (Jul 29)](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/321940/20260729/crypto-hacks-hit-all-time-high-north-korea-drains-over-600m-ai-agents-become-new-target.htm)

## 4. 🎯 Hunt hooks

**1. BNB Chain "publicly callable vault" sweep**
On any BNB Chain target, search for vault/treasury contracts with `transfer()`, `withdraw()`, `sweep()`, or `migrate()` functions that lack `onlyOwner`, `onlyRole`, or equivalent modifiers. Use the Etherscan/BscScan "Write Contract" tab to verify whether these functions are callable by any EOA. The Crypto DAO pattern is trivially discoverable via static analysis yet keeps recurring — this is low-hanging fruit for both attackers and bug bounty hunters.

**2. HTLC solver authorization in atomic-swap protocols**
On any HTLC-based bridge or DEX (THORChain, Maya Protocol, Garden Finance forks, OKX DEX HTLC layer), check whether the `claimHTLC(preimage)` or equivalent destination-chain function validates that the source-chain lock is still locked and unexpired **on-chain**, or whether it trusts an off-chain solver attestation. If the destination claim can be submitted by any address presenting a valid preimage without a linked source-lock proof, that is the Garden Finance bug class.

**3. Governance proposal payload inspection**
Following BonkDAO (Jul 6, $20M) and BarnBridge (Jul 28, $776K), any DAO with token-weighted governance and a delay < 48h should be treated as partially exploitable. When reviewing governance proposals as a hunter: decode the full calldata of all `execute()` targets — not just the human-readable description. Malicious actors consistently embed treasury drain calls in proposals described as "reward distribution" or "YES voter incentives."
