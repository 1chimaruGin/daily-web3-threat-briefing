# Web3 Threat Briefing — 2026-10-04

> **Scope:** Multi-chain (EVM/Solidity · Solana/Rust · Cosmos/Move · cross-chain bridges). Priority: DeFi, wallets, key infrastructure.
> **Window:** Last ~24–48h (October 2 – October 4 UTC). Two new incidents are both small (~$72K and ~$114K) and share a family resemblance — accounting logic that fails to isolate caller-supplied funds from protocol- or hook-injected liquidity within a single transaction. The more consequential news is corrective and confirmatory: a real root cause finally surfaced for Payy's bridge drain, Bitget's hack got a formal nation-state attribution, and a wallet-adjacent staking-infrastructure incident (MetaMask/Lido/Linea) that started Sept 30 is added to tracking for the first time.

## 1. 🔴 Active exploits (last 24h)

### MALT Stablecoin — Swap/Rebalance-Hook Accounting Bug, ~$72K

**Chain:** Polygon (MALT Stablecoin protocol; DAI-denominated treasury, referred to as the "Capital Source").
**Status:** CONFIRMED via SlowMist Threat Intelligence alert, Oct 3, 2026 at 18:44 UTC. Per aggregator reporting, the project says it has patched the flaw and will compensate affected users — no primary post-mortem from the MALT team was located.
**What happened / root cause:** MALT's `swap(uint256,uint256,address)` function records the caller's input and the pool's pre-swap reserves, then invokes an external `rebalanceHook` before transferring the requested output. That hook withdraws DAI from the protocol's own Capital Source and deposits it back into the same pool. The swap's final validity check then measures the pool's post-call balances without distinguishing caller-supplied funds from this treasury-funded top-up — so the hook's own liquidity injection gets counted as if the trader had supplied it. An attacker could submit a negligible real input, trigger the rebalance, and walk away with MALT priced against liquidity the treasury itself just added.
**Bug class:** logic-error / invariant-validation (failure to isolate caller-funded input from hook-funded capital within one call).

### GoldPesa GPXHooks — Uniswap v4 Hook Flash-Accounting Netting Exploit, ~$114K (alleged)

**Chain:** Base (GoldPesa's GPXHooks — a Uniswap v4 hook managing protocol-owned GPX/USDC liquidity with an automated rebalancing mechanism).
**Status:** ALLEGED / unconfirmed by GoldPesa. Reported by on-chain monitoring account Defimon Alerts on Oct 3, 2026; exploit transaction dated Oct 2, 2026 at 13:05:51 UTC. No official statement from the GoldPesa team was found as of this writing — treat as reported-but-unconfirmed.
**What happened / root cause:** The attacker borrowed 175,000 USDC from Morpho, opened an unpaid WETH/USDC position, then triggered GPXHooks' `reBalance()` function. When the hook burned its own liquidity position (recovering ~148,900 USDC), Uniswap v4's PositionManager netted that recovered amount directly against the attacker's outstanding debt inside the same PoolManager transaction — effectively forcing the hook's own liquidity to cover the attacker's borrow. The hook never isolated its rebalancing flow from external obligations open in the same transaction.
**Bug class:** logic-error (flash-accounting / cross-position netting failure in a Uniswap v4 hook).

**Sources:**

### Aave v3 — FlashLoopAdapter / Loop Safe Module Access-Control Exploit, ~114.09 ETH (~$305K) (Day 4)

**Status:** UNRESOLVED. ### Payy Network — Ethereum Rollup Bridge Drain, ~$1.92M USDC (Day 11) — *root cause now disclosed*

**Chain:** Ethereum (Payy Network / Payy Wallet non-custodial rollup; underlying proving stack built on Aztec's Noir/Barretenberg toolchain).
**Status:** Post-mortem actually published **Sept 29, 2026** — this was missed in the prior briefing window, which had reported it as still overdue; corrected here. Payy's investigation, validated with a third-party audit firm, found the Sept 24 drain was caused by a soundness bug in Barretenberg's small-subgroup IPA verifier: it was missing a boundary-opening check (A(1) = 0), which let the attacker submit an invalid burn proof that the verifier accepted as valid. Two burns — 1,828,589.378132 USDC and 90,202.820016 USDC — drained the rollup's pooled bridge liquidity to a single wallet. Aztec had already fixed this in commit 58bf73a (14 May 2026) and shipped it in v5.0.0 (13 Jul 2026), but Payy's deployment was pinned to an older build (bb 3.0.0-manual.20251030 / Noir 1.0.0-beta.14) and was never notified of the upstream fix. Payy explicitly ruled out compromised keys, social engineering, and off-chain-infrastructure compromise — this was purely a cryptographic verifier bug.
**Bug class:** zk-proof-soundness / supply-chain-dependency-drift (unpatched third-party ZK verifier, version pinning without upstream-advisory monitoring). Any other project still running pre-v5.0.0 Barretenberg/Noir inherits the same exposure until it upgrades.
**Caveat:** This root-cause account is corroborated across the same aggregator citing Payy's own Sept 29 X post; Payy's primary X thread could not be independently fetched for this briefing (blocked), and no major outlet (CoinDesk/Decrypt/The Block) was found independently covering the technical post-mortem — treat the specific commit hash and version strings as reported, not independently re-verified against Aztec's repo.

## 4. 🎯 What to hunt

**1. Hook/rebalance logic that fails to isolate protocol-injected liquidity from caller input (MALT, GoldPesa — both new this window)**

Two independent incidents, same shape: a swap or liquidity-management function calls an external hook mid-transaction (rebalance, netting, repayment), and the function's final balance check can't tell the difference between "the trader paid this" and "the protocol/hook just injected this." In MALT, treasury DAI injected by a rebalance hook got counted as caller-supplied collateral. In GoldPesa's Uniswap v4 hook, PositionManager's flash-accounting netted the hook's own recovered liquidity against an attacker's unrelated open debt inside the same PoolManager transaction. Any protocol using Uniswap v4 hooks, rebalancing callbacks, or multi-step "borrow → trigger external call → settle" patterns needs to verify: does the final invariant check attribute every unit of value to the actual party who supplied it, or just to "whatever the balance is now"? This is a distinct, underexamined corner of flash-accounting risk — not the classic flash-loan price manipulation, but same-transaction fund attribution.

**2. ZK proving-stack version pinning as a supply-chain risk (Payy/Aztec, root cause newly disclosed)**

Payy ran a soundness-critical verifier bug in production for roughly four months after Aztec silently fixed it upstream (May → Sept exploit), because Payy's deployment was pinned to an older Noir/Barretenberg build and nobody flagged the gap. Any project built on a third-party proving system (Aztec, RISC Zero, SP1, Halo2-based stacks, etc.) should treat "are we on the latest verifier release, and do we have a process for catching upstream advisories" as an active audit question — this is a repeatable failure mode wherever a security-critical dependency is vendored or pinned without a tracked upgrade path.

**3. Validator / staking-operator infrastructure as a wallet-adjacent attack surface (MetaMask/Lido/Linea, newly tracked)**

The theft here was trivial (~$1K), but the blast radius wasn't: a fee-recipient tampering bug at one staking operator forced ~$1.4B in ETH across two downstream protocols (Lido, Linea) into a multi-week exit-and-requeue cycle, with root cause still undisclosed five days in. For wallet/custody audits, map every staking integration a wallet offers — "non-custodial" doesn't mean the operator's validator configuration is outside your threat model; a compromise there can freeze or redirect yield even when private keys are never touched.

**4. Third-party adapters/modules bolted onto audited core protocols (Aave FlashLoopAdapter, Day 4)**

Unchanged: a helper contract trusting a cheap boolean (`isModuleEnabled`) as a stand-in for caller identity, paired with an arbitrary-call path. Also worth noting as a process pattern: the victim's on-chain whitehat bounty offer (90% return, 10% keep, hard deadline) is itself a recurring post-incident negotiation template worth being familiar with when triaging live incidents.

**5. Recovery-rate realism vs. attribution headlines (Bitget, Day 11)**

Formal DPRK attribution via Chainalysis doesn't change the recovery math — still ~$1.1M frozen of $387.5M. For exchange/custody risk assessments, attribution confidence and fund-recovery likelihood are separate axes; don't let a strong attribution story imply a strong recovery outlook.

**6. Post-mortem latency as its own signal, updated**

Payy's real post-mortem existed since Sept 29 but was missed in the prior window — a reminder that "report not yet published" claims age quickly and are worth re-checking rather than carrying forward by default. Meter.io, DCENT, Fetch.ai, MultiversX, Blink, GoldPesa and (less surprisingly, given it's hours old) MALT still have no primary team write-up.
