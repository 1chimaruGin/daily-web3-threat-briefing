# Web3 Threat Briefing — 2026-09-16

*Reconstructed on 2026-09-21 from archived sources; written after the fact, not same-day.*

> **Scope:** Multi-chain (EVM/Solidity · Solana/Rust · Cosmos/Move · cross-chain bridges). Priority: DeFi, wallets, key infrastructure.
> **Window:** September 15–16 UTC. **Headline: Sept 16 itself produced two mid-sized contract bugs — a Flamincome flash-loan share-price inflation and a Nimiq OpenGSN forwarder that shipped without its signature check. The window's two biggest stories both belong to Sept 15 and are carried forward at Day 2: the $7.73M Gnosis Safe rsETH drain (an attacker-reachable custom module with DELEGATECALL rights, front-run in the same block by a generalized MEV searcher), and the DCENT App Wallet sweep — which the vendor first disclosed publicly on the 16th, hours after it had already finished.**

## 1. 🔴 Active exploits (last 24h)

### Flamincome (legacy contracts, Flamingo Finance) — Flash-Loan Share-Price Inflation (Sep 16)

**Chain:** Ethereum
**Status:** Exploited; affected contracts are described as **older/legacy Flamincome deployments**, not current Flamingo Finance production vaults.
**Lost:** ~**$345,900**

**What happened:** An attacker took an **~$18M USDT flash loan**, then injected USDP/3CRV LP into a legacy Strategy contract. The Strategy treated a **permissionlessly injectable Convex `BaseRewardPool` balance as its own assets** and valued it via Curve's `get_virtual_price()` against a **depegged pool** — overstating the position. That inflated the `VaultYUSDT` share price, and the attacker redeemed real Aave `aUSDT` liquidity at the inflated rate for ~$345.9K profit.

**Contracts named in the Blockaid alert:**
- Victim: `0x0461eEFF7C856020E574c0c364FE968Ca06BCc0F`
- Abused token: `0xb8d6471cA573C92c7096Ab8600347F6a9Fe268a5`

The Crypto Times notes that the initial public notice contained "no further technical breakdown of the pricing mechanism or the precise accounting step that permitted the inflation" — the mechanism above comes from the alert summary and should be treated as the reported reconstruction rather than a published post-mortem.

**Detection:** Blockaid. Also logged twice by SlowMist for the same date under "Smart Contract Vulnerability" and "Flash Loan Price Manipulation."

**Bug class:** flash-loan, price-manipulation (share-price inflation via permissionless balance donation + unsafe `get_virtual_price()` on a depegged pool)

### Nimiq — OpenGSN Forwarder Missing Signature Verification (Sep 16)

**Chain:** Polygon
**Status:** Nimiq suspended Gas Abstraction stablecoin trading and opened an investigation.
**Lost:** ~**$50,400** (SlowMist logs $50,463)

**What happened:** Nimiq's exchange contract acted as **both paymaster and forwarder for OpenGSN**, and its `execute` function **did not verify user signatures**. The attacker stood up their own GSN relay, forged open requests originating from the project's liquidity wallet, redeemed immediately after unlocking the HTLC with a preimage hash lock, and transferred out USDC, USDT0 and USDC.e.

This is the cleanest bug of the day and the most reusable lesson: a meta-transaction forwarder whose sole security property is signature recovery, shipped without the signature check.

**Detection:** Defimon Alerts.

**Bug class:** signature-verification / access-control (trusted forwarder accepting unsigned requests)

**Sources:**
- [OKX Orbit — Nimiq exchange contract exploit analysis (via Defimon Alerts)](https://www.okx.com/en-us/orbit/insight/87713771195840)
- [SlowMist Hacked database — Nimiq, $50,463](https://hacked.slowmist.io/)

## 2. 🟠 Ongoing — carried forward

### Unidentified Gnosis Safe — rsETH Drained via Module-Authorization Abuse (Day 2)

**Chain:** Ethereum
**Status:** Funds extracted **Sep 15**; receiving address still under the Kelp DAO 24-hour wallet-level pause applied that morning. Victim not publicly identified.
**Sep 16 development:** no new on-chain movement and no vendor statement. What is new in this window is third-party corroboration — SlowMist logged the incident in its Hacked database at **$7,730,000**, and PeckShield independently verified the approximate figure, firming up a number that was a press estimate on the 15th.
**Lost:** ~2,900 rsETH — reported as **~$7.73M** (SlowMist) / **~$7.8M** (press).

**Recap:** At 04:38:47 UTC on **Sep 15**, in Ethereum block 25,980,525, a Gnosis Safe v1.3.0 at `0x40E93a52F6Af9fCD3b476aeDADD7FeABD9f7AbA8` was drained of roughly 2,900 rsETH.

The attack path did **not** involve owner keys or a Safe core flaw. Security firm Blockaid characterised it as "module-authorization abuse on that Safe, not a Safe core / owner-key bug." A **custom liquidity-provider module** enabled on the Safe exposed an unguarded "recipe" entrypoint that forwarded caller-supplied calldata into the Safe's `execTransactionFromModuleReturnData` with DELEGATECALL privileges. Because enabled modules bypass the owner-signature threshold entirely, anyone who could reach that entrypoint inherited the Safe's full authority.

Funds were routed through a **Uniswap v4 pool carrying an attacker-controlled hook**, which unwrapped `aEthrsETH` into freely transferable rsETH.

**The MEV twist:** the exploit transaction was broadcast to the public mempool and never settled to the exploiter. A generalized MEV searcher publicly tagged **"Yoink"** (EOA `0xFDe0d1575Ed8E06FBf36256bcdfA1F359281455A`) executed first in the same block, captured 2,882.37 rsETH to `0xC70f00CD7E461686b04B0E912E309becA8b80ea0`, and swapped the residual 17.63 rsETH through Uniswap v4.

**Response:** At 06:03 UTC Kelp DAO flagged the receiving address and applied a temporary 24-hour pause preventing rsETH from moving in or out of it. Kelp stated rsETH "remains fully backed, and all operations – minting, withdrawals, and integrations – are running normally." Its core contracts were not involved.

**Bug class:** access-control (module authorization / unguarded DELEGATECALL forwarder)

**Sources:**
- [The Crypto Times — $7.8M rsETH Drained From an Ethereum Safe Wallet. An MEV Bot Front-Ran the Exploit in the Same Block.](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/09/15/7-8m-rseth-drained-from-ethereum-safe-wallet-mev-bot-yoink-front-runs-the-exploit/)
- [SlowMist Hacked database — "Unknown Gnosis Safe Wallet", $7,730,000](https://hacked.slowmist.io/)

### DCENT App Wallet — Signing Vulnerability, Abnormal Asset Transfers (Day 2)

**Chain:** Multi-chain — Bitcoin, Ethereum, XRPL, TRON and other EVM chains
**Status:** ACTIVE / vendor investigation ongoing. Root cause deliberately withheld.
**Incident date:** the sweep itself ran on **Sep 15**, 16:29–18:34 UTC (01:29–03:34 KST on Sep 16) — on-chain analysis and SlowMist both date it to the 15th. **The Sep 16 development carried here is the vendor's first public disclosure**, which did not exist while the drain was running.
**Lost:** **Unconfirmed.** DCENT has published **no loss figure**. SlowMist's incident database logs **$6,570,000** with attack method "Unknown." Later third-party reporting (Sep 17 onward, i.e. outside this window) circulated substantially larger and mutually inconsistent XRP figures. Treat every number here as unconfirmed.

**The Day 2 development:** DCENT (formerly D'CENT), a South Korean wallet vendor, received its first report in the early morning KST of September 16 — hours after the sweep completed — and published its first public notice the same afternoon KST. The notice stated the company had "detected abnormal asset transfers involving the DCENT App Wallet and are currently conducting an urgent investigation."

**Scope, per DCENT's own preliminary incident report:**
- Affected: **App Wallet (software wallet) on app versions earlier than 8.1.0** (released 2025-11-05), on both Android and iOS
- Affected chains: Bitcoin, Ethereum, XRPL, TRON, EVM-based chains and others
- Affected addresses: those with **"a history of sending (signing)"** — this is the key qualifier, and it points the finger at the signing path rather than at seed generation or storage
- Hardware wallets (Biometric, DCENT X, DCENT S) are affected **only** where a user imported the hardware recovery phrase into the App Wallet. DCENT: "So far, we have not confirmed any impact on hardware wallets."

**Root cause:** Not disclosed. DCENT explicitly declined, stating that "if specific technical information were made public, it could be used in identical or similar attacks or lead to further harm." The company titled its report "App Wallet Signing Vulnerability," which is the only public characterisation of the flaw class. Nothing further is confirmed — do not infer a specific nonce or RNG failure from the title alone.

**User action DCENT published:**
1. Update the DCENT app to **version 10.0.0 or later** immediately
2. Verify recovery-phrase backups before acting
3. Transfer all App Wallet assets to a **new wallet created with a different recovery phrase**
4. Never reuse a potentially compromised recovery phrase
5. Do not transact on older app versions; ignore unsolicited transfer instructions from unofficial channels

**Bug class:** key-management / signature-verification (vendor-designated "signing vulnerability"; specifics withheld)

### Liquid Network / Elements — Rangeproof Cache-Key Collision (Day 11)

**Chain:** Liquid (Bitcoin sidechain) / Bitcoin
**Status:** Chain operational — block production resumed September 10 after a four-day halt. The incident remains open on the **recovery** track only: **598.5 BTC (~$47M) is still unreturned.**
**Day count:** Day 11, counted from first public disclosure on **September 6, 2026**.

**Sep 15–16 developments:** None confirmed. I found no new public statement from Blockstream, the federation or the attacker in this window. The state of play is unchanged from the September 11 position described below.

**Recap of the incident:** On September 6, 2026 at 15:53:10 UTC (Liquid block 4,050,336), an actor exploited an **ambiguous cache-key encoding in the Elements rangeproof verification cache** to mint **3,998.5 unbacked L-BTC (~$318.7M)** and peg them out to native Bitcoin, draining roughly 95% of Liquid's reserves in about 23 minutes.

**Root cause (CertiK reconstruction):** the cache implementation bound entries to four fields — rangeproof (P), value commitment (C), asset generator (X), and script (S) — but "fed the raw fields into one SHA-256 stream without encoding their lengths." Because both P and S are variable-length, distinct tuples could produce an identical byte stream by shifting bytes across field boundaries. The published collision example:

```
Valid primer:  (P0,  C0,  X,  6a43 || C1 || X || 6a)
Attack input:  (P0 || C0 || X || 6a43,  C1,  X,  6a)
```

The attacker primed the cache with setup transactions, then submitted a malicious inflation transaction that hit the poisoned cache entry and skipped real verification.

**Patch:** **Elements v23.3.4**, released September 9, adds length prefixes to cache keys and introduces a `-norangeproofcache` emergency switch to disable the cache entirely. Reviewed with the Bitcoin Red Team and Alpen Labs. **No CVE identifier has been assigned** as far as I can establish.

**Recovery status:** The actor communicated via Bitcoin `OP_RETURN` messages declaring "we are whitehats," returned 3,400 BTC after Blockstream patched its bridge nodes, and retained 598.5 BTC as a self-declared bounty, demanding a further 10% payout from Blockstream's own funds. On **September 11** Blockstream publicly rejected the demand, framing it as theft rather than disclosure, and said it would involve law enforcement, exchanges and forensic specialists. CEO Adam Back stated the 1:1 L-BTC/BTC peg will be covered by Blockstream, removing the leverage the demand rested on.

**Bug class:** logic-error (length-extension-style hash collision in a verification cache key)

**Sources:**
- [CertiK — Liquid Network Incident Analysis](https://www.certik.com/blog/liquid-network-incident-analysis)
- [The Crypto Basic — Blockstream Rejects Liquid Hackers' 10% Bounty Demand as 598 BTC Remains Unreturned](https://thecryptobasic.com/2026/09/11/blockstream-rejects-liquid-hackers-10-bounty-demand-as-598-btc-remains-unreturned/)

## 3. 🟢 Advisories & patches

**No new web3-specific CVE was published in the September 15–16 window** that I could confirm. Two items are worth acting on regardless:

### Elements < v23.3.4 — rangeproof verification cache collision (no CVE assigned)

**Affected:** Elements node software prior to **v23.3.4** | **Patched:** v23.3.4 (September 9, 2026)
**Severity:** Critical — permits minting of unbacked assets on any Elements-derived chain that uses the rangeproof verification cache.
**Action:** Upgrade to v23.3.4 or later. Operators who cannot upgrade immediately can run with `-norangeproofcache` to disable the cache path entirely at a performance cost.
**Note of record:** the underlying fix was committed publicly to the Elements repository on **September 1, 2026 — five days before the exploit** — with no release containing it deployed to production nodes at the time of the attack. Anyone running an Elements fork or downstream sidechain should verify they are on a patched build.
**Source:** [CertiK — Liquid Network Incident Analysis](https://www.certik.com/blog/liquid-network-incident-analysis)

### DCENT App Wallet < 10.0.0 — signing vulnerability (no CVE assigned, details withheld)

**Affected:** DCENT App Wallet on versions earlier than **8.1.0**; DCENT directs all users to **10.0.0 or later**
**Severity:** Treat as critical for any affected seed. DCENT's guidance is migration to a **new seed**, not merely an app update — which implies key material is considered compromised, not just the client.
**Action:** Update to ≥ 10.0.0, then move funds to a wallet generated from a fresh recovery phrase. Any hardware wallet whose phrase was ever typed into the App Wallet is in scope.
**Source:** [DCENT — Preliminary Incident Report](https://store.dcentwallet.com/blogs/post/app-wallet-incident-report)

## 4. 🎯 What to hunt

**1. Safe modules are the soft underbelly — enumerate `enabledModules`, not owners**

The rsETH drain is the highest-value reminder this window: a Safe's security is the union of its owner threshold **and** every enabled module, and modules bypass the threshold completely. Anything reachable through `execTransactionFromModule` / `execTransactionFromModuleReturnData` runs with the Safe's full authority and no signature check.

For any treasury, DAO, LP manager or vault operator built on Safe:
- Enumerate enabled modules on-chain. Custom, one-off and "LP helper" modules are rarely audited to the same standard as the Safe itself and are the ones to read.
- In each module, find every externally-callable entrypoint that forwards **caller-supplied calldata or a caller-supplied target** into the Safe. A "recipe," "batch," "multicall" or "route" function is the canonical shape.
- Confirm the access control on that entrypoint. Missing `onlyOwner`/`onlySafe`, an allowlist that is never populated, or a check on `msg.sender` that an attacker can satisfy, all collapse to full Safe compromise.
- Treat DELEGATECALL forwarding from a module as critical by default — it lets the attacker rewrite the Safe's own storage.

**2. Meta-transaction forwarders: verify the verifier (Nimiq)**

Nimiq lost funds because an OpenGSN forwarder's `execute` did not check the user signature. This is a mechanical, greppable bug class and it keeps shipping:
- For any contract implementing `IForwarder`, ERC-2771, a custom relayer, or an account-abstraction paymaster, read `execute`/`verify` and confirm `ecrecover` (or an EIP-1271 `isValidSignature` call) is actually **performed and its result checked** — not computed and discarded, not gated behind a flag that defaults off.
- Confirm the nonce is incremented and bound into the signed digest, and the domain separator includes `chainid` and the verifying contract.
- Watch specifically for contracts that are **both paymaster and forwarder**, as Nimiq's was. Merging the two roles tends to collapse the trust boundary the signature check was defending.
- Then check what the forwarded call can reach: a liquidity wallet, an HTLC redeem path, or any privileged operator address downstream turns a signature-check miss into direct fund loss.

**3. Share-price inflation via permissionless balance donation (Flamincome)**

The Flamincome pattern is the classic ERC-4626-family inflation bug with two amplifiers. Sweep vault and strategy code for:
- Any accounting that counts a balance an outsider can **push into the contract without permission** — a Convex `BaseRewardPool` stake, a raw `transfer` into the strategy, a rewards claim anyone can trigger. If `totalAssets()` reads a live balance rather than an internally tracked figure, it is donatable.
- Direct use of Curve's `get_virtual_price()` (or any pool-derived price) with no **depeg guard**. The attack here depended on the pool being off-peg; a sanity band around the expected price would have blunted it.
- **Legacy deployments.** These were older Flamincome contracts, not current production. Protocols that migrate leave the old vaults live and unmonitored, with residual approvals and liquidity still reachable. When scoping a target, explicitly ask which prior-generation contracts are still deployed and still hold or can pull funds — that surface is routinely excluded from audits and routinely still exploitable.

**4. Hash-derived cache keys over variable-length fields (Elements)**

The Liquid bug generalises well beyond Elements. Anywhere a verification result, signature check, or authorization decision is memoised under a key built by concatenating variable-length byte fields into one hash stream, distinct inputs can collide by moving bytes across field boundaries. Look for `sha256(a || b || c)` / `keccak256(abi.encodePacked(...))` with **two or more dynamic-length arguments** and no length prefixes — `abi.encodePacked` with multiple `bytes`/`string` parameters is the Solidity-native instance of exactly this bug. If the resulting hash gates a "we already verified this" shortcut, a collision means skipping verification entirely.
