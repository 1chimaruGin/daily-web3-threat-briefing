### Bitget — Backend Authorization Spoofing, ~$387.5M (Day 5)

**Chain:** Multi-chain exchange infrastructure (Ethereum, BNB Chain, Avalanche, TRON, Zcash — ETH, BNB, AVAX, USDT, USDT0, USDC, XAUT, XRP, ZEC, TRX reported)
**Status:** ONGOING recovery (Day 5, from **Sept 24** detection). Bitget's phased withdrawal-resumption schedule hit its first milestone: **Bitcoin withdrawals resumed at 08:00 UTC on Sept 28**, preceded by a CEO Gracy Chen AMA at 07:30 UTC. ETH withdrawals (across Ethereum, BSC, Arbitrum, Base, Optimism) are next at 08:00 UTC Sept 29, USDT at 08:00 UTC Sept 30, and everything else (other tokens, fiat, P2P) at 08:00 UTC Oct 2. New this window: Bitget launched a **stolen-fund recovery bounty program** — 5% of any successfully frozen amount and a further 5% of any successfully recovered amount, payable to whoever's voluntary action directly caused the freeze/recovery (court-ordered freezes are excluded; freezes secured before the announcement still qualify). Separately, on-chain investigator **AMLBot** traced roughly **4 BTC** of the stolen funds being laundered through a **Wasabi CoinJoin** round — the funds' path was TRX→USDT→bridged to Ethereum via USDT0→~145 ETH→THORChain→~4.59 BTC→split and fed into CoinJoin. AMLBot notes this is a small fraction of the total: **~$343M (~88%)** of the tracked ~$389M in stolen assets has still not moved at all. **A full technical root-cause report has still not been published**; Bitget says the underlying vulnerability "has been identified and remediated" (attackers reportedly forged transaction data that Bitget's own backend then treated as trustworthy input to its normal signing/approval flow — not a stolen private key), and continues to cite North Korea-linked IP/VPN infrastructure as supporting evidence, which remains company-alleged rather than independently forensically confirmed.
**Bug class:** access-control (backend transaction-authorization/validation bypass via spoofed transaction data feeding a trusted signing pipeline).

## 4. 🎯 What to hunt

**1. Forged/spoofed "trusted input" into an otherwise-legitimate signing or approval pipeline (still the Bitget lesson)**

The clearest technical detail to emerge this window: Bitget's attackers didn't steal a key — they compromised a backend component that *fed* transaction data into the exchange's normal signing/approval flow, and that flow trusted the data as genuine. For any custody, multisig, or MPC signing system in scope, ask not just "is the key protected?" but "what generates the message the signers/approvers are shown, and can that generation step be compromised independently of the key material?" Multisig and hardware-isolated signing provide no protection if the thing describing the transaction lies to them.

**2. Marketplace/UI-level mitigations papering over unpatchable contract bugs**

OpenSea's decision to flag 3,000+ NFTs as untradeable is a sensible stopgap, but it's cosmetic relative to the underlying bug — Payment Processor V2 remains callable by anyone, forever, on every chain it's deployed to, and any wallet with a still-live legacy approval stays exposed regardless of what any single marketplace does. When auditing NFT/DeFi infrastructure, check whether "the marketplace flagged it" is being treated internally as equivalent to "the vulnerability is fixed" — it is not, and any other integrator of the same unpausable contract remains fully exposed.

**3. Fund-recovery bounty programs as a new attacker/whitehat incentive surface**

Bitget's 5%/5% freeze-and-recovery bounty is the first instance in this series of an exchange formalizing a reward for third-party fund tracing after a hack. Worth watching whether other 2026 incidents (Neutron, Payy, Meter.io — all still lacking recovered funds) adopt similar programs, and whether bounty terms create perverse incentives (e.g., disputes over who "directly caused" a freeze when multiple trackers flag the same address).

**4. Post-mortem latency as its own signal**

Five of the eight carried-forward incidents (Bitget, Neutron, Payy, Meter.io, Blink) are now 5+ days old with no published technical root-cause report, despite public commitments to deliver one "in the next few days" in several cases. For bug-bounty and audit purposes, don't wait on official post-mortems to start reasoning about root cause from on-chain evidence — by the time Payy's or Neutron's reports land, the underlying bug class (rollup proof-verification gaps; chain-governance override of app-level admin controls) will already be public knowledge via independent analysis, and contracts sharing that same pattern are exploitable today.

**5. Governance-cost-to-TVL ratio in Cosmos-SDK/CosmWasm chains — still unresolved, still no post-mortem (Neutron, Day 6)**

Unchanged from prior briefings: for any CosmWasm protocol in scope, calculate the ratio between the cost of winning a governance vote at spot price right before tally and the value of assets that vote's outcome can control, and check for timelocks between proposal tally and execution. `MsgUpdateAdmin`-style chain-governance overrides of app-level multisig protection are a durable pattern, not a one-off.

**6. Client-side signing implementations in hardware/software wallets — treat early figures as a floor (DCENT, Day 14)**

DCENT's XRP leg has now run two full weeks with the signing-compromise mechanism still undisclosed. Prioritize signing/key-derivation code review in any hardware or mobile wallet audit, and don't anchor on a project's first-reported loss number — this one grew roughly 3x from its initial estimate.
