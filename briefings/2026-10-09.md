### MakerDAO — Dormant ETH-A Liquidation Keeper Proxy Drained, ~200 WETH (~$538K) (Day 4)

**Chain:** Ethereum. A third-party keeper proxy contract used to automate MakerDAO ETH-A collateral liquidation auctions — not MakerDAO's own Vat/Flipper/GemJoin core contracts.
**Status:** Closed, single transaction. Exploit executed Oct 6, 2026, 06:13:11 UTC. Funds hit the attacker's wallet within a minute and began Tornado Cash mixing (split into 10 ETH batches) roughly six minutes later. MakerDAO's core protocol was unaffected; the flaw sat entirely in the deprecated third-party keeper component.
**What happened / root cause:** The keeper proxy (address 0x9c05…8273) had won four liquidation auctions back during the March 2020 "Black Thursday" crash (auctions #1457-1460, 50 WETH each) with zero bids, but had never actually settled/paid for them. Its collateral-withdrawal function — selector `0x8804d1de` — lacked any access-control (`ds-auth`) check, so any caller could invoke it directly and settle those four abandoned 2020 auctions, extracting the 200 WETH the keeper was never entitled to.
**Bug class:** access-control (missing authentication on a legacy third-party keeper's exit/drain function; the bug had been dormant for roughly six years).

### Set Protocol — Legacy ETHMACOAPY `actualizeFee()` Rounding Exploit, ~8.11 WETH (~$21.9K) (Day 4)

**Chain:** Ethereum. Legacy Set Protocol product ETHMACOAPY (an old TokenSet, not Set's current maintained stack); Uniswap v4 flash accounting was used to supply temporary liquidity for the attack.
**Status:** Closed. Two separate attacks on Oct 6, 2026 (UTC+8), the second (4.062 WETH) nearly exhausting the Set's remaining reserves.
**What happened / root cause:** `actualizeFee()` is a public function, gated only by the Set being in its "Default" rebalance state — no owner or governance role required. It calls `handleFees()` (which mints nothing when no fee is actually due) and then unconditionally overwrites `unitShares` via `calculateNewUnitShares()`, which uses `divCeil` and therefore always rounds up. The attacker issued Set shares, triggered `actualizeFee()` to bump `unitShares` upward even though zero fee was owed, then redeemed the same share quantity — extracting more collateral per share than they had deposited, funded by a flash-borrowed Uniswap v4 position to scale the extraction.
**Bug class:** logic-error / invariant-validation (permissionless fee-recalculation function that rounds in the caller's favor across separate deposit/redeem calls) — the same bug family as GoldPesa's hook-rebalance netting and MALT's accounting miscount, below.

**Sources:**

**Europol: exposed-public-key crypto wallets are the primary quantum-attack surface, not blockchains (Oct 7).** Europol's European Cybercrime Centre (EC3) published two reports concluding that a sufficiently powerful quantum computer would most plausibly derive private keys from already-exposed public keys via Shor's algorithm, rather than attack blockchain consensus itself — and separately warned about "harvest now, decrypt later," where adversaries archive today's on-chain public-key data to decrypt once such a computer exists. Europol's bottom line: cryptocurrencies won't collapse from quantum computing, but wallet/protocol developers should start testing post-quantum algorithms and migration plans now rather than waiting.

**Sources:**

**Ethereum Foundation researcher warns AI math progress — not quantum computers — may break ECDSA first, urges "bunker mode" (Oct 8).** Justin Drake pointed to OpenAI's Oct 6 release of 722 AI-generated mathematical results (spanning algebra, theoretical computer science and mathematical logic) as evidence that rapid AI-driven progress in mathematics could crack ECDSA — the signature scheme securing most Bitcoin and Ethereum accounts — faster than quantum computing matures, in his worst case "months not years." He defined a "break" as recovering a private key in about a week using available hardware (e.g., a large GPU cluster), and recommended a calmly-paced, controlled migration of assets to fresh addresses that have never signed a transaction, starting with sophisticated/high-value holders. Vitalik Buterin publicly agreed the risk should be taken seriously but cautioned against hasty, panicked migrations. This is a speculative, forward-looking risk assessment, not an announced break — no ECDSA key has been demonstrated recovered via this method as of this writing.

## 4. 🎯 What to hunt

**1. Permissionless fee/share-recalculation functions that round in the caller's favor (Set Protocol, new concrete example this window)**

Set Protocol's `actualizeFee()` is the cleanest example yet of a bug class this briefing has now seen three times in two weeks (MALT, GoldPesa, Set Protocol): a function anyone can call, gated only by a coarse state check (not a role), that recomputes a global accounting value (unit shares, pool balance, rebalance ratio) using a rounding mode that favors whoever calls it next. For any vault, index, or structured product: check every public function that mutates a share-price or unit-accounting variable — who can call it, what rounding direction it uses, and whether a caller can sandwich their own deposit/redeem around that call.

**2. Unprotected exit/drain functions on dormant third-party integrations (MakerDAO keeper, new this window)**

A six-year-old bug: a keeper bot's withdrawal function had no `ds-auth` check and sat untouched since 2020 until someone finally called it. For any protocol with third-party bots, keepers, or adapters bolted onto its core (even ones the core team doesn't maintain or control) — dormancy is not safety. Check whether *any* integration still holds assets from a feature, auction, or flow that hasn't been used in years, and whether its exit path actually checks who's calling.

**3. A known bug with no reporting channel is still an unpatched bug (Base vault, standing)**

Immunefi's head of security says the Base vault's whitelist weakness was reportedly known to researchers a week before the $6M exploit, with no clear disclosure channel to the (still-unidentified) team. Before spending time on a target, check whether it has an actual working disclosure path.

**4. Governance-controlled recovery has layers, and each layer can fail differently (Neutron, now with two distinct proposals)**

Prop 1056 (refund the attacker) failed on quorum before the veto even mattered. Prop 1058 (route the recovered ATOM to a Neutron-side multisig for victim distribution, not to the attacker) is a structurally different ask and is still in voting. Don't assume a single failed governance vote means "recovery is dead" — check whether a narrower, differently-scoped follow-up proposal exists before concluding that.

**5. Multisig whitelist/approval toggles as an attack primitive (Base vault, standing)**

Two consecutive, validly-signed Safe transactions (remove, then re-add 60 seconds later) that no one claims to have intended, after 25 days of dormancy. Check who can change a whitelist, how fast changes take effect, and whether any delay exists between a whitelist change and usability.

**6. Recovery-rate realism vs. attribution headlines (Bitget, Day 16)**

Still only ~$1.1M frozen of $387.5M despite formal DPRK attribution. Treat attribution confidence and fund-recovery likelihood as separate axes.

**7. Wallet/key-infrastructure teams should have a post-quantum and "AI-math" migration story, even if neither risk is imminent (new, advisory-stage)**

Europol's wallet-focused quantum report and Justin Drake's AI-math warning are both speculative/forward-looking, not active exploits — but both converge on the same practical question for wallet and custody audits: does this wallet have any path to migrate a user to a fresh key without the user needing to understand why? Worth a line item in any wallet security review, independent of how seriously you weight the underlying risk timeline.

**8. Post-mortem latency as its own signal (standing)**

Meter.io, DCENT, Fetch.ai, MultiversX, Blink, MALT and now GoldPesa still have no primary team write-up, several weeks overdue. Reason from on-chain evidence and third-party PoCs (DeFiHackLabs, SlowMist) rather than waiting — but keep re-checking, since real technical pictures have repeatedly emerged well after initial, thinner reporting this cycle.
