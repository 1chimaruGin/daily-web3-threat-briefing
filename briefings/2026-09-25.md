# Web3 Threat Briefing — 2026-09-25

> **Scope:** Multi-chain (EVM/Solidity · Solana/Rust · Cosmos/Move · cross-chain bridges). Priority: DeFi, wallets, key infrastructure.
> **Window:** Last ~24–48h (September 23–25 UTC). **Headline: Bitget confirmed a ~$351.6M breach of its hot and warm wallets — provisionally the largest crypto-exchange hack of 2026 — with an unverified North Korea/Lazarus attribution from the CEO and a root-cause report still pending. A second, smaller hot-wallet compromise hit gambling platform Duelbits the same day. Both are key-management incidents, not smart-contract bugs — a reminder that exchange and platform hot-wallet infrastructure remains the highest-yield target in the ecosystem right now.**

## 1. 🔴 Active exploits (last 24h)

### Bitget — Hot/Warm Wallet Breach, ~$351.6M (figure disputed)

**Chain:** Multi-chain exchange infrastructure — assets drained across Ethereum, BNB Chain, Avalanche and others (ETH, BNB, AVAX, USDT, USDT0, USDC, XAUT reported)
**Status:** CONFIRMED breach, UNCONFIRMED root cause and UNCONFIRMED total. Detected **Sept 24, 18:31 UTC**; Bitget activated emergency response within minutes and suspended withdrawals (deposits and trading remained open). CEO Gracy Chen publicly stated the company "believes" North Korea's Lazarus Group was responsible, citing IP addresses linked to VPN services associated with the group and pattern similarities to prior attacks — **this attribution is alleged, not confirmed**; no named security firm or independent forensic report has corroborated it as of this writing, and Chen's stated basis includes a personal prior incident, not necessarily forensics specific to this breach. A full incident report including root-cause analysis is promised before **Sept 25, 21:30 UTC** (within 24h of detection) — not yet published at the time of writing.
**Impact:** Bitget's own figure is **~$351.6M**. Independent on-chain tracking (Arkham Intelligence) had confirmed only **~$183.8M** moved across roughly 13 linked addresses at the time of the official announcement, rising toward ~$190M per Bubblemaps shortly after; one outlet also flagged an unconfirmed additional ~$143M XRP-denominated leg. **Treat $351.6M as the company-reported ceiling, not an independently verified figure**, pending Bitget's own report. Cold wallets are explicitly reported as unaffected (a widely-shared headline suggesting cold wallets were hit is not supported by the CEO's own statement). Bitget says its ~$464M user-protection fund covers the full claimed loss.
**What happened:** Between roughly 18:31 and 20:55 UTC on Sept 24, a newly created address withdrew tens of millions of dollars in stablecoins (including ~19.67M USDT0) from Bitget-labeled hot wallets and rapidly swapped them into ETH — including one swap on Arbitrum executed well above market price, consistent with urgency over slippage. The attacker's cluster consolidated stolen stablecoins and other assets into ETH (freezable-to-unfreezable conversion, a standard laundering step) and moved funds across multiple chains before the exchange's public confirmation.
**Root cause:** Not yet disclosed. Bitget says it will not speculate on the attack vector before the investigation concludes. No forged-withdrawal-request or customer-private-key-extraction claim has been made; the company frames this as unauthorized hot-wallet transfers, consistent with either a compromised hot-wallet signing key/infrastructure or an insider/operational failure — undetermined pending the promised report.
**Bug class:** key-management (hot/warm wallet signing infrastructure compromise — exact vector undisclosed).

### Duelbits — Suspected Hot-Wallet Private-Key Compromise, ~$4.2-4.3M

**Chain:** Ethereum + BNB Chain (gambling platform's multi-chain hot wallets)
**Status:** CONFIRMED suspicious outflow, UNCONFIRMED root cause. Detected via PeckShield/ScamSniffer alerts starting **Sept 24, ~09:00 UTC**; PeckShield's figure is ~$4.3M, ScamSniffer's is ~$4.2M — a small but real discrepancy between trackers. Duelbits itself had not issued a public statement on the incident as of this writing, and it is unconfirmed whether customer balances were directly affected.
**Impact:** ~$4.2-4.3M across Ethereum and BNB Chain: 836 ETH (~$2.23M), 1.146M USDT, 209 BNB (~$160.7K), 96,805 USDC, 12.398B SHIB (~$70.2K), and 31,515 DAI. The attacker has already consolidated proceeds into roughly 1,588 ETH and moved funds through cross-chain bridges (deBridge, Relay Router V3).
**What happened:** Multiple transfers from Duelbits-linked wallets to a newly created attacker address occurred between 09:00:35 and 09:31:23 UTC on Sept 24, immediately followed by conversion to ETH and bridging — the same freeze-resistant-asset-consolidation pattern seen in the Bitget incident hours later.
**Root cause:** Undisclosed. PeckShield explicitly stated it could not determine how the attacker gained wallet access. ScamSniffer's characterization is "suspected private key compromise," consistent with Duelbits' prior February 2024 incident (~$4.64M, also attributed to loss of wallet access control), but this is pattern-matching, not a confirmed root cause for the current event.
**Bug class:** key-management (hot-wallet private key compromise — vector unconfirmed).

### Neutron Governance Takeover — Astroport + Drop, ~$9.4M (Day 3)

**Chain:** Cosmos (Neutron consumer chain; Cosmos Hub spillover)
**Status:** ONGOING (Day 3, counted from **Sept 23** first public disclosure/reporting; attack executed ~Sept 22 night ET / Sept 23 02:24 UTC). Cosmos Hub resumed block production at **12:00 UTC Sept 23** after a ~24h48m halt, moving ~1.227M ATOM (~$2.1-2.2M) out of the attacker-linked wallet on the first post-restart block — though a follow-up report notes an additional ~168,990 ATOM escaped via a THORChain refund that hit the attacker's wallet after the restart. **Neutron's own block-production resumption is still not independently confirmed** as of this writing; the project has not yet published the full post-mortem it promised once back online.
**Root cause (unchanged):** Neutron's `wasmd`-based governance can invoke `MsgUpdateAdmin` to override any CosmWasm contract's app-level admin/multisig — the attacker spent ~$20K in NTRN, acquired 11 minutes before a vote tally, to seize control of contracts holding $9.4M via a disguised proposal.
**Bug class:** access-control / governance-privilege-escalation; economic-design (governance-attack cost far below protected-asset value).

## 4. 🎯 What to hunt

**1. Hot/warm-wallet signing infrastructure at exchanges and custodial platforms**

Two independent hot-wallet compromises (Bitget, Duelbits) surfaced within hours of each other on Sept 24, both showing the same signature: rapid multi-asset withdrawal, immediate conversion of freezable stablecoins into ETH, and cross-chain bridging within minutes of the first outflow. Neither root cause is public yet. If scope includes exchange or custodial-platform infrastructure, this is the moment to push on: hot-wallet key custody (HSM vs. software signing, key-rotation cadence, who/what can co-sign a withdrawal), withdrawal-request authentication paths, and whether any automated monitoring can detect and freeze an anomalous outflow inside the 2+ hour window these incidents took to notice and react.

**2. Don't take exchange-reported loss figures at face value until independently traced**

Bitget's own $351.6M figure was roughly double the ~$183.8M that on-chain trackers (Arkham) had independently confirmed at announcement time. This gap is common in early hours of an incident — track both the company claim and independent on-chain tracing separately, and expect the gap to close (or the company figure to be revised) as the promised root-cause report lands.

**3. Chain-level governance authority overriding app-level admin/multisig controls (Cosmos/CosmWasm) — still unresolved**

Neutron's `MsgUpdateAdmin` privilege-escalation path (Day 3, ~$9.4M, no post-mortem yet) remains the clearest live lesson: calculate the ratio between the cost of winning a governance vote at spot price right before tally and the value of assets that vote's outcome can control, for any Cosmos-SDK/CosmWasm protocol in scope. Check for timelocks between proposal tally and execution.

**4. Client-side signing implementations in hardware/software wallets — blast radius still growing**

DCENT's XRP-only tracing (Day 11, ~$18.7M) remains the standing example: treat any "preliminary" loss figure attached to a client-side key-compromise incident as a floor, not a ceiling, and prioritize signing/key-derivation code review in any hardware or mobile wallet audit.

**5. VM/client-layer atomicity and shared bridge-key blast-radius — still open**

MultiversX's VM-level atomicity bug (Day 7, restart still undetermined) and the Fetch.ai/SingularityNET shared bridge-key incident (Day 7) remain live, unresolved bug classes in production. Standing guidance unchanged: enumerate every contract/token/chain trusting a shared authorizer key before scoping a bridge audit as "one converter, one key."
