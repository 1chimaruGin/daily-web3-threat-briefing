### Base Vault (protocol unidentified) — Safe-Whitelist Re-Toggle Exploit, ~$6M wstETH stolen (+ ~$31.7M at risk) (Day 5)

**Chain:** Base (Ethereum L2). Vault contract (a TransparentUpgradeableProxy), controlled by a 3-of-7 Safe multisig. The vault lent through Aave V3 on Base; Aave's own core contracts and Base itself were not compromised.
**Status:** Still unclaimed — as of Oct 7, more than 72 hours after the exploit, no team had publicly claimed ownership of the vault, per GoPlus Security and Immunefi. New this window: Gonçalo Magalhães, Immunefi's Head of Security, said the underlying flaw — that any Safe-approved address on the whitelist could borrow against the vault's Aave position without posting collateral — had reportedly been identified by researchers roughly a week before the exploit, but there was no clear disclosure channel to report it to the (still-unidentified) responsible team. No post-mortem exists, and no public evidence has surfaced showing whether the Safe's re-whitelisting was approved via compromised signer keys, a manipulated signing workflow, or insider collusion.
**Root cause:** Unchanged — unconfirmed. Both Safe transactions (de-whitelist, then re-whitelist 60 seconds later) carried valid signer signatures, so the failure sits upstream of the Safe's cryptographic logic, in an authorization process that remains undisclosed.
**Bug class:** access-control (multisig/whitelist authorization-process compromise; exact vector undisclosed); separately, a responsible-disclosure-channel gap per Immunefi.

### Aave v3 — FlashLoopAdapter / Loop Safe Module Access-Control Exploit, ~114.09 ETH (~$305K) (Day 8)

**Chain:** Ethereum (third-party "Loop Safe Module" adapter used alongside Aave v3 and Safe multisig wallets; Aave's and Safe's own core contracts unaffected).
**Status:** UNRESOLVED, now five days past the Oct 3, 18:00 UTC whitehat-bounty deadline (keep 11.41 ETH, return 102.69 ETH). No public confirmation found of whether the attacker accepted, returned funds, or let the offer lapse.
**Root cause (unchanged):** FlashLoopAdapter's `open()`/`close()` functions verified callers only via `ISafe(msg.sender).isModuleEnabled(address(this)) == true` — spoofable with a forged Safe contract that always returns true — combined with an unrestricted arbitrary-call `_swap()` path.
**Bug class:** access-control (trusting a module-enabled check as caller-identity proof, plus unrestricted arbitrary-call path).

### GoldPesa GPXHooks — Uniswap v4 Hook Flash-Accounting Netting Exploit, ~$114K (Day 6)

**Chain:** Base (Uniswap v4 hook).
**Status:** Still no official statement from GoldPesa, but the technical mechanism is now well-documented by independent third parties: SlowMist's TI alert puts the loss at ~$114,999.99 USDC gross (~$114,428.51 net), and a reproducible proof-of-concept was published to the DeFiHackLabs exploit archive on GitHub. Per that analysis, GPXHooks rebalances GoldPesa's protocol-owned GPX/USDC liquidity inside `beforeSwap()` once an hour has elapsed since the last rebalance; the attacker borrowed 175,000 USDC via Morpho, opened a position through `PositionManager.modifyLiquiditiesWithoutUnlock` (`BURN_POSITION` + `TAKE_PAIR`) without repaying it, left ~115,000 USDC of debt against the PositionManager, then triggered a swap that fired the hourly rebalance — netting the attacker's unpaid debt against the pool's own liquidity. No admin, signer, or privileged role was involved in the attacker's path.
**Bug class:** logic-error (flash-accounting / cross-position netting failure in a Uniswap v4 hook's time-triggered rebalance callback).

## 4. 🎯 What to hunt

**1. A known bug with no reporting channel is still an unpatched bug (Base vault, new angle this window)**

Immunefi's head of security stated publicly that the Base vault's whitelist weakness — any address added to the list could borrow against the vault's Aave position without posting collateral — was reportedly identified by researchers roughly a week *before* the $6M exploit, but there was no clear channel to disclose it to a team that, to this day, hasn't identified itself. For hunters: before spending time on a target, check whether it actually has a working disclosure path (a listed bug-bounty program, a security contact, a monitored inbox). A real vulnerability sitting in a void with no owner to report to is a race against whoever finds it next — and this time, the attacker won that race.

**2. Multisig whitelist/approval toggles as an attack primitive, not just a config action (Base vault, still unclaimed)**

Unchanged from prior editions: two consecutive, validly-signed Safe transactions (remove attacker contract from whitelist, re-add it 60 seconds later) that no one claims to have intended. For any audit touching a Safe-gated vault, lending whitelist, or module-permission list: check who can change the list, how fast changes can be made after a long dormancy period (this Safe had been dormant 25 days), and whether there's any delay/timelock between a whitelist change and that address becoming usable.

**3. Hook rebalance callbacks that let a caller leave unpaid debt before the callback fires (GoldPesa, now technically well-documented)**

GoldPesa's GPXHooks shows the concrete shape of this bug class: a Uniswap v4 hook's `beforeSwap()` rebalances protocol-owned liquidity on a timer (once per hour), and a caller can flash-borrow, open a PositionManager position via `modifyLiquiditiesWithoutUnlock` without repaying it, then trigger the swap that fires the rebalance — netting their own unpaid debt against the pool's liquidity, with no privileged role required anywhere in the path. Any protocol running Uniswap v4 hooks with time- or state-triggered rebalancing should check whether the rebalance can observe and settle against debt a caller left open in the same transaction (or even the same block).

**4. Recovery-rate realism vs. attribution headlines (Bitget, Day 15)**

Still only ~$1.1M frozen of $387.5M despite a formal DPRK attribution. Attribution confidence and fund-recovery likelihood remain separate axes for any exchange/custody risk assessment.

**5. Governance-controlled recovery is not the same as recovery (Neutron, Day 16 — now with a concrete failure mode)**

Prop 1056 didn't just lose the vote — it failed on quorum before the vote split even mattered (19.94% turnout vs. a 40% bar). For any protocol whose incident-response plan assumes "we'll just pass a governance proposal to fix it": low voter turnout can kill a recovery plan procedurally, even when the sentiment among those who do vote is overwhelming. Worth checking what quorum a target chain's recovery-relevant proposals actually need, and whether turnout historically clears it.

**6. Third-party adapters/modules bolted onto audited core protocols (Aave FlashLoopAdapter, Day 8)**

Unchanged: a helper contract trusting a cheap boolean (`isModuleEnabled`) as a stand-in for caller identity, paired with an arbitrary-call path. The victim's on-chain whitehat bounty offer (90% return, 10% keep, hard deadline) remains a useful template to know — the deadline is now five days gone with no public resolution either way.

**7. Post-mortem latency as its own signal (standing)**

Meter.io, DCENT, Fetch.ai, MultiversX, Blink and MALT still have no primary team write-up, several now weeks overdue. Don't wait on official write-ups to reason from on-chain evidence; also don't assume "no write-up yet" means "nothing happened" — re-check periodically, since both Payy's and GoldPesa's real technical pictures emerged well after initial, thinner reporting.
