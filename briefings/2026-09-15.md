# Web3 Threat Briefing — 2026-09-15

*Reconstructed on 2026-09-21 from archived sources; written after the fact, not same-day.*

> **Scope:** Multi-chain (EVM/Solidity · Solana/Rust · Cosmos/Move · cross-chain bridges). Priority: DeFi, wallets, key infrastructure.
> **Window:** Last ~24–48h (September 14–15 UTC). **Headline: September 15 was a wallet-layer day, not a protocol-layer day. Neither of the two significant incidents was a bug in a DeFi protocol's core contracts — one was a third-party module a Safe owner had authorized, the other was a wallet vendor's own app. The most interesting technical detail is that the Safe exploit was front-run in the same block by an MEV bot, turning a theft into a three-way race.**

## 1. 🔴 Active exploits (last 24h)

### Safe (Gnosis Safe) Custom Module — Authorization Bypass, ~2,900 rsETH (Sep 15)

**Chain:** Ethereum
**Status:** Funds moved; Kelp DAO applied a 24-hour wallet-level pause on the receiving address at 06:03 UTC. rsETH itself remained fully backed and Kelp's core contracts were not touched.
**Impact:** ~2,900 rsETH. USD figure is **not settled** — Blockaid and PeckShield estimates span **$7.73M–$7.81M**, and CoinDesk reports "roughly $7.8 million." There is no official reconciled figure accounting for all follow-on transactions.

**What happened:** At 04:38:47 UTC in block 25980525, an attacker drained a Safe v1.3.0 wallet (`0x40E93a52F6Af9fCD3b476aeDADD7FeABD9f7AbA8`) that had authorized a custom liquidity-provider module built to automate a Uniswap v4 position. The attacker routed a leveraged rsETH position (held as `aEthrsETH` on Aave V3) through an attacker-controlled Uniswap v4 pool whose hook unwrapped the tokens into freely transferable rsETH, then moved them out.

**The front-run:** An MEV bot tagged on-chain as **Yoink** (`0xFDe0d1575Ed8E06FBf36256bcdfA1F359281455A`) spotted the exploit transaction in the public mempool and executed in the *same block*, paying roughly 18.93 ETH (~$46–47K) in priority fees to the builder. Yoink captured 2,882.37 rsETH and routed it to `0xC70f00CD7E461686b04B0E912E309becA8b80ea0`, swapping the residual ~17.63 rsETH through Uniswap v4. Follow-on movement went through Fluid Dex and other venues, outside protocol-level control.

**Root cause:** A flawed authorization check in the module's Multicall path. The module exposed an ungated entrypoint that forwarded caller-supplied calldata into the Safe's `execTransactionFromModuleReturnData` with DELEGATECALL privileges — the permission check approved any caller that named the helper contract itself as the target, so signature requirements were bypassed entirely. Blockaid characterised it explicitly as "module-authorization abuse on that Safe, not a Safe core / owner-key bug." BlockSec, Blockaid, SlowMist and AstraSec all placed the flaw in the owner-authorized component, not in Safe's core contracts.

**Unconfirmed:** the Safe owner's identity, the final loss figure, any statement from the Yoink operator, and any vendor statement on the module itself.

**Bug class:** access-control (unauthenticated module entrypoint + DELEGATECALL), mev-frontrun

### D'CENT App Wallet — Mass Automated Sweep, ~2.0M XRP (Sep 15)

**Chain:** XRP Ledger (vendor states other chains are also in scope — Bitcoin, Ethereum, TRON, EVM chains)
**Status:** ACTIVE / UNRESOLVED as of the window's close. D'CENT did not publish its first urgent notice until the afternoon of Sep 16 KST, meaning the sweep ran and completed before any public vendor warning existed.
**Impact:** **2,009,321 XRP from 1,552 wallets.** USD figure varies by source — The Crypto Basic puts it at ~$2.8M at time of theft; other outlets report "over $2.5 million." Treat the exact dollar figure as unconfirmed.

**Timeline (UTC, Sep 15):**
- 16:29–16:44 — first automated wave
- 16:44–17:17 — 33-minute pause, apparently manual drainage
- 17:17–18:34 — second wave; total elapsed 2h 05m
- 20:18 — first stolen funds reach an exchange

**Root cause:** **Not disclosed.** D'CENT's own incident report explicitly declines to publish technical details, stating that doing so "could be used in identical or similar attacks or lead to further harm." What the vendor *has* published is the scope criteria, which is the useful signal: affected wallets are those whose recovery phrase was **entered into the App Wallet at any point**, which have signing history, and which used app versions **prior to 8.1.0** (released November 5, 2025). That scoping is consistent with a signing/entropy or seed-handling defect in the app, but the vendor has not confirmed a mechanism and no third party has independently established one.

**Hardware wallets:** D'CENT states hardware devices are not themselves affected — with the explicit exception that a hardware recovery phrase which was at some point typed into the App Wallet **is** in scope.

**Independent analysis (unconfirmed):** The Crypto Basic reports that the attack order correlated 0.65 with wallet *creation date* but only 0.09 with *balance size*, which is consistent with the attacker working from a pre-compiled list of compromised keys rather than scanning the ledger live. That is an inference from on-chain ordering, not a vendor confirmation. ~118,400 XRP was reportedly moved through the Bridgers cross-chain service.

**Attribution:** None. No security firm or law-enforcement body has publicly attributed this incident.

**Bug class:** key-management (vendor-side, mechanism undisclosed)

## 2. 🟠 Ongoing — carried forward

### SpiralCom / SpiralHookV2 — Spot-Price Collateral Manipulation, ~10.7 ETH (Day 2)

**Chain:** Ethereum (Uniswap v4 integration)
**Status:** Closed, small. The exploit landed **Sep 14** and is carried forward here because the pattern matters more than the amount. **No new development was reported in the Sep 15 window** — state-of-play, not news.
**Impact:** ~10.7 ETH (~$26,800 as recorded by SlowMist) — a minor loss.

**Recap:** SpiralCom's `SpiralHookV2` integration valued collateral off the Uniswap v4 pool's **live spot price with no bound on how far that price could move**. An attacker used multiple EOAs to distort the spot price and extract value against the inflated valuation, all within a single Ethereum block.

**Important scoping:** this is a third-party integration flaw, not a defect in Uniswap v4's core protocol. The full set of affected contracts beyond those named is unconfirmed.

**Bug class:** price-manipulation (unbounded single-block spot oracle in a v4 hook integration)

**Source:** [The Cryptonomist — Uniswap V4 Exploit Exposes Key SpiralCom ETH Loss](https://en.cryptonomist.ch/2026/09/14/uniswap-v4-exploit-spiralcom/)

### Tectonic / Cronos — Day 17 (exploit Aug 30) · rekt.news post-mortem published Sep 14

No new on-chain activity in this window, but the detailed public post-mortem landed **September 14** and firms up figures that were rough at the time of the incident.

**Confirmed figures:** $120.4M borrowed across nine Tectonic markets from ~$5M of initial capital. Cronos validators halted at block **90,907,150** (14:32:47 UTC, Aug 30) and restarted from **90,896,189**, discarding **10,961 blocks** (1h 54m of chain history). The rollback recovered **$111.2M**; **$9.19M** had already bridged to Ethereum and remains unrecovered.

**Root cause:** Tectonic assigned a 20% collateral factor to its own governance token TONIC despite ~$1.34M liquidity and ~$11K daily volume. The attacker looped deposit-and-reborrow **98 times** to accumulate a collateral claim near two-thirds of TONIC's supply, then bought TONIC on the open market to move its reported price ~195× — note earlier reporting said "~100× in 20 minutes," and the post-mortem's ~195×/~300× framing is the revised figure.

**Bug class:** price-manipulation (illiquid self-token accepted as collateral), logic-error (unbounded recursive looping)

### Symbiosis Bitcoin Bridge — Day 5 (disclosed Sep 11)

**Status:** Native Bitcoin Bridge **still offline** as of Sep 13, with no restart timeline published. Other Symbiosis cross-chain routes operate normally; BTC swaps are being served via Chainflip and THORChain.

### Osmosis / Nomic allBTC — Day 7 (disclosed Sep 9; underlying exploit Jun 25)

**Status:** allBTC minting, redemption, deposits and withdrawals frozen since Sep 9. Governance proposal pending to restore backing.

A double-spend in Nomic's custom forwarding logic minted ~40.65 unbacked nBTC on **June 25** — and went undetected for **74 days**, leaving Osmosis's alloyed BTC roughly **36% unbacked** (~$3.15M short). An emergency upgrade locked 22.65 BTC in attacker addresses; the proposed remedy confiscates that and covers the remaining ~17.19 BTC shortfall from the Osmosis community pool.

The 74-day detection gap is the finding here, not the dollar amount: an alloyed/wrapped asset had no continuous proof-of-backing check against its upstream bridge.

**Bug class:** logic-error (double-spend in bridge forwarding), monitoring gap

### Liquid Network (Blockstream) — Day 10 (exploit Sep 6)

**Status:** Block production resumed; vulnerability patched. **~598 BTC (~$47M) remains outstanding** of the ~4,000 BTC (~$320M) drained. The attackers self-describe as white hats and returned 3,400 BTC after the fix shipped — their motive and identity are **not confirmed**.

**Root cause:** a flaw in **Elements**, the software Liquid runs on, permitting creation of unbacked L-BTC that was then redeemed for real BTC through SideSwap's peg-out desk. No federation multisig key was compromised — the bug sat upstream of the 11-of-15 signing federation, in the transaction validation path.

**Bug class:** logic-error (validation bypass permitting unbacked issuance)

## 3. 🟢 Advisories & patches

### CVE-2026-57583 — OpenZeppelin Contracts Wizard comment-breakout code injection (LOW)

**Affected:** `@openzeppelin/wizard` ≤ 0.10.10, `@openzeppelin/wizard-cairo` ≤ 3.0.0, `@openzeppelin/wizard-stellar` ≤ 0.6.1, `@openzeppelin/wizard-stylus` ≤ 0.3.0
**Patched:** 0.10.11 / 3.0.1 / 0.6.2 / 0.3.1 respectively
**Severity:** LOW — CVSS 3.3 (`CVSS:3.1/AV:L/AC:L/PR:N/UI:R/S:U/C:N/I:L/A:N`)
**Advisory:** GHSA-9wxg-vf3r-56hc

**What it does:** Values supplied for `info.securityContact` or `info.license` are interpolated verbatim into single-line comments of generated Solidity, Cairo, Stellar/Soroban or Stylus source. A line terminator in those fields closes the comment, so everything after it is parsed as real source declarations. The fix adds validation in `setInfo` rejecting line terminators.

**Why the severity is genuinely low, and when it is not:** normal self-service use — web UI, AI assistant, CLI, self-hosted API — does not cross a trust boundary, and shared Wizard links cannot set these fields. No code executes on any Wizard service. The exploitable case is narrow but real: an **integration that populates these fields from untrusted input** (the advisory calls out an AI agent processing external content) and a human who then consumes the generated contract. If you run Wizard programmatically inside a codegen pipeline that takes third-party metadata, treat this as more than a 3.3.

**Date note:** the CVE record was published **September 14, 2026**, which is what puts it in this window. The underlying GitHub advisory page carries an earlier date (reported as June 18, 2026), so this is a late CVE assignment to an already-published advisory, not a fresh disclosure.

**Sources:**
- [GitHub Advisory GHSA-9wxg-vf3r-56hc](https://github.com/OpenZeppelin/contracts-wizard/security/advisories/GHSA-9wxg-vf3r-56hc)

## 4. 🎯 What to hunt

**1. Safe/Gnosis modules are the soft underbelly — audit the module, not the wallet**

The Sep 15 incident is the cleanest recent example of a pattern that keeps paying: the wallet was fine, the *thing the wallet trusted* was not. Enabled Safe modules can call `execTransactionFromModule` / `execTransactionFromModuleReturnData` with **no signature check at all** — the module *is* the authorization. So for every enabled module on a high-value Safe:

- Find every external/public entrypoint. Does each one gate on `msg.sender`, or does it gate on something the caller controls — like the *target address* of the call being made? The Sep 15 module approved any caller that named the helper itself as the target. That is a check that looks like a check.
- Does any path forward **caller-supplied calldata** into the Safe's module-exec functions? If so, the module has effectively delegated the Safe's full authority to the public.
- Is `DELEGATECALL` (operation = 1) reachable from a caller-influenced parameter? That converts a limited module into arbitrary control of Safe storage.
- Strategy-executor, auto-compounder, keeper and "liquidity manager" modules are the highest-yield category: they are written to be called by a bot, so the authorization tends to be an afterthought, and they are attached to Safes holding real size.

Safes with Uniswap v4 hook automation are a specific target right now — the hook gives the attacker a controllable unwrap/settle primitive inside the same transaction.

**2. Wrapped/alloyed asset backing needs a continuous invariant, not an incident response**

Osmosis went **74 days** with allBTC 36% unbacked. The bug was upstream in Nomic, but the detection failure was local. When reviewing any wrapped, alloyed, or multi-bridge-aggregated asset:

- Is there an automated, continuous check that `totalSupply(wrapped) <= verified_backing(upstream)`? Or is backing assumed because the bridge said so?
- For aggregator assets that pool *several* bridged versions of the same underlying, a single compromised constituent silently dilutes the whole basket — is per-constituent backing tracked, or only the aggregate?
- Does the mint path re-verify the upstream proof, or trust a forwarding/relay message? Nomic's flaw was in **custom forwarding logic** permitting a double-spend — forwarding and relay glue is under-audited relative to the mint contract itself.
- Same question for unbacked-mint paths in bridges generally: the Symbiosis BridgeV2 issue was in the same family, and its notional was $46.1B against $336K realized. The gap between notional and realized is about liquidity, not about the severity of the bug.

**3. Price-bound your oracle reads, especially inside Uniswap v4 hooks**

SpiralCom lost only ~10.7 ETH, but the shape is the same one that cost Tectonic $120.4M: collateral valued off a manipulable live price with no ceiling on movement. Sweep for:

- Collateral or solvency logic reading `slot0`/spot price from a v4 pool with no TWAP, no deviation bound, and no per-block price-movement limit
- Protocols accepting their **own governance token** as collateral — then compare the collateral factor against the token's actual liquidity and daily volume. Tectonic's 20% factor sat against ~$1.34M liquidity and ~$11K daily volume. That ratio is the finding.
- Recursive deposit-and-reborrow loops with no cap on iterations or on aggregate position share of token supply. The Tectonic attacker looped 98 times from ~$5M of capital.

**4. For wallet vendors and anyone integrating them: scope by seed-handling history, not by product line**

D'CENT's scoping criteria are worth copying as a threat model. The blast radius was not "users of product X" — it was "any recovery phrase that has **ever** been entered into the app," including hardware-wallet seeds typed in once. If you maintain or integrate a wallet SDK, the question is: can a seed that transited a software surface at any point in its history be distinguished from one that never did? If not, a single app-side defect invalidates every key that ever touched it, including keys the user believes live only on hardware.
