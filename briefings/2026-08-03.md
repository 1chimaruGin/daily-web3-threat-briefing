### Coldcard Firmware PRNG — Wave 3 Laundering Phase Begins — $89M Cumulative (Day 4)

**Chain:** Bitcoin  
**Cumulative losses (waves 1–3):** ~1,367 BTC / ~$89M  
**Addresses drained:** 4,585 (unchanged overnight — no confirmed Wave 4 as of 08:00 UTC Aug 3)  
**Status as of today:** Wave 3 P2WSH outputs (1,912 addresses, ~$13.5M) are sitting unspent. The attacker appears to be consolidating rather than immediately laundering — consistent with operational-security delay observed in the July 30 Wave 1 first-movement pattern. Galaxy Research is monitoring the P2WSH script hashes for any spend activity; chain-analytics clustering will fail until redemption scripts reveal on spend.

**Root cause (for newcomers):** A March 2021 firmware build error silently routed seed generation in Coldcard devices to a deterministic software PRNG (MicroPython Yasmarang) instead of the STM32 hardware RNG, collapsing effective entropy to ~40 bits on Mk3 devices, ~72 bits on Mk4/5/Q. Seeds generated under affected firmware versions (v4.0.0–v5.0.3 confirmed; additional ranges under investigation) are computationally brute-forceable.

**Remediation — still urgent for anyone not yet patched:**
- **Mk3 (FW 4.0.1–5.0.3):** No firmware patch exists. Move all funds to a new seed on different hardware **immediately.** ~40-bit entropy is brute-forceable at scale by current GPU clusters.  
- **Mk4 / Mk5 (before FW 5.6.0) / Q (before FW 1.5.0Q):** Apply Coinkite emergency firmware, then rotate any seed that was generated *before* patching.  
- **BIP39 passphrase + 50+ dice rolls** at seed generation time provides meaningful additional entropy; confirmed unexploited addresses in the drains shared this pattern.  
- Updating firmware does **not** fix an already-generated compromised seed.

**Bug class:** key-management

### BlueNoroff ClickFix Campaign — Full Technical Disclosure — 100+ Crypto Victims

**Attribution:** North Korea / DPRK — BlueNoroff (financially motivated subgroup of Lazarus Group)  
**Active campaign window:** May 31 – July 14, 2026 (5 kit versions released; campaign may continue under new domains)  
**Victims:** 100+ confirmed across 20+ countries; 41% in United States  

**How it works:**
1. Target receives invitation via compromised Telegram account or typosquatted Calendly link to a fake Zoom or Microsoft Teams meeting.
2. Fake platform (one of 80+ lookalike domains registered since late 2025) prompts victim with a fake error and instructs them to paste a PowerShell command ("ClickFix" social engineering — victim self-installs malware).
3. **Before dropping malware**, the kit scans the victim's browser for installed crypto wallet extensions (MetaMask, Phantom, Ledger Live, etc.) and the number/value of accounts. Low-value targets are abandoned without malware delivery — the campaign selectively targets high-value holders.
4. If the target clears the value threshold, the kit establishes persistence, disables Microsoft Defender via exclusion policies, performs system reconnaissance via WMI queries, and exfiltrates stored wallet credentials, seed phrases, and session tokens.
5. The Teams variant is more advanced than Zoom: supports emoji reactions, blocks mobile devices, and performs deeper wallet checks before execution.

**Key OPSEC indicator:** If a team member reports attending a Zoom or Teams meeting that "glitched" and asked them to run a pasted command — treat this as a confirmed ClickFix attempt and perform a full endpoint forensic scan immediately.

**Bug class:** key-management (social engineering vector → credential theft)

### XRPL xrpld 3.3.0 — Two Security-Patched Amendments Returning This Week

**Context:** The XRP Ledger's upcoming xrpld 3.3.0 release — expected to reach validators this week — resubmits two amendments previously withdrawn over security flaws, now patched.

**BatchV1_1 (replacement for withdrawn Batch):**  
Security researcher Pranamya Keshkamat (via Cantina) found that the original Batch amendment's signature validation could allow unauthorized transactions to slip through — an attacker submitting a crafted batch could bypass authorization checks on individual sub-transactions. The fix changes the amendment ID to BatchV1_1 (preventing old validators from accidentally activating the vulnerable version), removes the early-exit condition, and adds additional authorization guards at each sub-transaction boundary.

**Permission Delegation (resubmitted after September 2025 withdrawal):**  
Original withdrawal reason: potential vulnerabilities that could drain accounts. No full public post-mortem was issued, but the pattern described fits a delegation-scope escalation — a delegated signer exceeding their authorized scope to drain the delegating account. The patched version includes additional scope enforcement.

**Activation:** Requires 80% validator approval for two consecutive weeks — neither amendment is live yet. If you audit any XRPL-integrated protocol that plans to use Batch transactions or Permission Delegation after activation, review the new amendment specs for scope-boundary assumptions before they go live.

### CVE-2026-40072 — web3.py SSRF via CCIP Read / EIP-3668 (Day 20 — broadly unpatched)

**Affected library:** `web3` (Python/PyPI) — versions < 7.15.0 (stable) and < 8.0.0b2 (beta)  
**Severity:** HIGH  
**Vector:** `handle_offchain_lookup` and `async_handle_offchain_lookup` perform HTTP requests to attacker-controlled gateway URLs embedded in smart contracts, with zero URL validation. This enables SSRF to AWS IMDSv1, GCP/Azure metadata endpoints, internal RPC nodes, and private APIs from any backend process calling these functions — meaning any backend relying on CCIP Read for off-chain data resolution is a potential SSRF pivot.  
**Fix:** `pip install "web3>=7.15.0"`  
**Still unpatched 20 days after disclosure** — this affects every Python-based oracle, relayer, or monitoring service that hasn't pinned the library.

## 4. 🎯 Hunt hooks

**1. Does any target protocol use Coldcard Mk3 as an admin or guardian signer?**  
Wave 3 confirmed the attacker is sweeping the entire vulnerable address space, not cherry-picking high-value targets. If a bug bounty target's admin multisig, upgrade guardian, or pause key was generated on a Coldcard Mk3 running firmware v4.0.1–5.0.3, the corresponding private key carries only ~40 bits of effective entropy — brute-forceable with a modest GPU cluster. Cross-reference: ask in protocol Discords what hardware key derivation was used for deployment keys, or check if on-chain signers are P2WPKH Bitcoin addresses (potential Coldcard-derived). Even a 3-of-5 Gnosis Safe is materially weakened if two or more Mk3-derived keys are among the signers.

**2. Audit your team for BlueNoroff ClickFix infection right now.**  
BlueNoroff's campaign ran through at least July 14, 2026, and had 100+ confirmed victims — crypto teams are the explicit target. Do a quick survey today: (a) check whether any team member attended a Zoom/Teams meeting in the past 60 days that prompted them to paste a command; (b) run an EDR scan for Yasmarang or PRNG-related detections (false positive hedge — different topic but same IoC namespace); (c) look for unexpected Microsoft Defender exclusion policies in group policies or local policy files on team Windows machines; (d) check PowerShell history (`Get-PSReadLineOption | select -exp HistorySavePath`) for any IEX, Invoke-Expression, or curl-to-pipe patterns from meeting dates. A single infection on a developer workstation with wallet access or deploy keys is a critical finding for any protocol you're hunting on.

**3. For XRPL-integrated protocols: review Batch transaction handling before 3.3.0 activates.**  
If your target integrates with XRPL and plans to support Batch transactions or Permission Delegation once 3.3.0 activates, audit now — not post-activation. The withdrawn original Batch amendment had a signature validation bypass that allowed unauthorized sub-transactions to execute. Even though the fix is in BatchV1_1, the pattern to check in *your* integration layer is: does your application independently validate sub-transaction authorization, or does it blindly trust the Ledger-level Batch result? An integration that trusts Ledger results without validating individual inner-transaction authorization could be exploited if a future amendment regression reintroduces the gap.
