# Web3 Threat Briefing — 2026-09-17

*Reconstructed on 2026-09-21 from archived sources; written after the fact, not same-day.*

> **Scope:** Multi-chain (EVM/Solidity · Solana/Rust · Cosmos/Move · cross-chain bridges). Priority: DeFi, wallets, key infrastructure.
> **Window:** Sept 16–17 UTC. **Headline: One fresh loss and a good day for post-mortems. Nostra's Starknet money market was drained of $3.5M in a 27-minute oracle pump that did not touch a single contract bug — the attacker hijacked the price *aggregator's* pool-selection heuristic. The other three entries are older incidents that finally became explicable on the 17th: working PoCs landed for both the Sept 15 Gnosis Safe rsETH drain (a nested self-referential `multicall` bypassing its own authorization check) and the Sept 16 Flamincome NAV inflation, and DCENT published its preliminary incident report on the Sept 15 App Wallet sweep.**

## 1. 🔴 Active exploits (last 24h)

### Nostra Finance — NSTR Oracle Manipulation via Aggregator Pool Hijack (Sept 17)

**Chain:** Starknet
**Status:** MONEY MARKET FULLY PAUSED — supply, borrow, withdrawal and liquidation all disabled while the team reconciles pool balances.
**Lost:** ~$3.5M borrowed against worthless collateral. ~$1.92M bridged out to Ethereum mainnet (234.57 ETH + 1.3M DAI); ~$1.55M remained on Starknet at time of reporting.

**What happened:** Between roughly **05:23 and 07:08 UTC on Sept 17**, an attacker inflated the reported price of Nostra's own governance token NSTR from about **$0.006 to $49.50 — roughly 8,000x** — and borrowed a basket of ETH, STRK, USDC, USDT, WBTC and DAIv1 against it.

Reconstructed sequence:
1. **05:23** — attacker creates a fake **NSTR/SolvBTC** pair seeded with ~1.5 SolvBTC, one-sided.
2. **05:27–05:47** — wash trades through the thin pool.
3. **05:47–05:48** — repeated swaps print the inflated NSTR price; the fake pool is selected as the reference venue.
4. **05:48–05:50** — borrows the ~$3.5M asset basket against the inflated collateral.
5. **05:51–07:08** — dumps proceeds across multiple DEXs and bridges out.

The attacker had reportedly accumulated NSTR and positioned collateral **months earlier, in March and August**, before executing in September.

**Root cause:** This is a **pricing-authority failure, not a smart-contract bug.** Nostra's price path relied on a third-party aggregator (GeckoTerminal), and the attacker hijacked that aggregator's **pool-selection logic** by standing up a new, near-empty pool that the heuristic then treated as the canonical venue. Compounding factors: NSTR was accepted as collateral with **no isolation mode**, **no borrow cap relative to market cap**, and no TWAP. NSTR's entire market cap was about **$546,751** — the borrowed basket was roughly **six times larger than the whole token**.

**Attribution:** None. No named group; the attacker wallet is described only as "one account" in the protocol's own statement.

**Note on timing:** Sources differ slightly. Two independent write-ups place the attack window at 05:23–07:08 UTC on Sept 17; one outlet cites 13:28 UTC Sept 17 from Nostra's statement (likely the pause, not the attack); PeckShield's public alert landed 00:41 UTC on Sept 18; AMBCrypto's piece dates the event Sept 18. **The Sept 17 date is consistent across the protocol's own statement and multiple trackers; the precise intra-day timestamp is not fully settled.**

**Bug class:** price-oracle-manipulation (third-party aggregator pool-selection hijack; no TWAP, no borrow cap, no isolated collateral mode)

## 2. 🟠 Ongoing — carried forward

### Gnosis Safe rsETH Drain — Nested Self-Referential Multicall Authorization Bypass (Day 3)

**Chain:** Ethereum
**Status:** Funds gone; not recovered. Victim is a single Safe, not a protocol treasury contract — no protocol-wide pause. Drain executed **Sept 15** at 04:38:47 UTC.
**Sept 17 development:** the mechanism became public. DeFiHackLabs merged a working PoC (PR #1262) reconstructing the exact authorization bypass — the first time the *nested self-referential multicall* path was published rather than described as generic "module-authorization abuse." The reconstruction below is new on the 17th; the drain is not.
**Lost:** ~2,900 aEthrsETH, reported as **~$7.73M–$7.8M** in realized rsETH.

**The newly published mechanism:** The Router's `multicall(address _contract, bytes[] data)` gates on an `_isAuthorized` check that **short-circuits to `true` whenever `_contract == address(this)`**. The attack chains that trick twice:

1. **Outer** `multicall(ROUTER, [...])` — passes authorization because the target *is* the Router.
2. The nested payload therefore executes **with the Router's own trusted identity**.
3. **Inner** `multicall(MODULE, [...])` — the enabled Safe module trusts the Router, so from inside that borrowed context the call is authorized.
4. The Safe module performs a **`DELEGATECALL`** into attacker-crafted data against the victim Safe.
5. Proceeds were routed through a malicious **Uniswap V4 hooked pool**: aEthrsETH injected, swapped, redeemed as rsETH.

An MEV bot named "yoink" front-ran roughly $47,000 out of the exit before 2,882 rsETH moved on.

**Victim Safe:** `0x40e93a52f6af9fcd3b476aedadd7feabd9f7aba8`
**Attack tx:** `0x0e7680b06cb8a6f86c149d9ba90d98e3d334e7b072dde03909d43fcfd98a8705` (block 25,980,525)

**Note on date — resolved:** the drain is a **Sept 15** event. Block 25,980,525 carries the attack transaction at 04:38:47 UTC on Sept 15, Kelp DAO applied its receiving-address pause at 06:03 UTC the same morning, and SlowMist Zone, CoinDesk and The Crypto Times all date it to the 15th. The Sept 16–17 dates attached to this incident come from the **DeFiHackLabs pull request**, which was opened on Sept 17 — a PoC publication date, not an incident date.

**Bug class:** access-control (self-referential authorization short-circuit → trusted-context laundering → DELEGATECALL)

### DCENT App Wallet — Abnormal Asset Transfers, Root Cause Undisclosed (Day 3)

**Chain:** Multi-chain (mobile software wallet)
**Status:** ACTIVE INVESTIGATION. Vendor has issued an evacuation advisory; no technical post-mortem published. The drain itself ran **Sept 15**, 16:29–18:34 UTC; DCENT's first public notice followed on **Sept 16**.
**Sept 17 development:** DCENT published its *Preliminary Incident Report* (dated Sept 17 KST), which names the flaw class — "App Wallet Signing Vulnerability" — and publishes the version and signing-history scoping criteria below, while still withholding the mechanism.
**Lost:** **Unconfirmed.** IoTrust (the vendor behind DCENT) has disclosed **no figure at all**. SlowMist Zone logs the incident at **~$6.57M with attack method "unknown"**, dated Sept 15 — treat that number as a third-party tracker estimate, not a confirmed loss.

**Recap:** On **Sept 16**, DCENT posted its first public notice: *"We have detected abnormal asset transfers involving the DCENT App Wallet and are currently conducting an urgent investigation."* Preliminary findings point to the issue being **limited to the App Wallet (software)**, with no confirmed compromise of DCENT hardware wallets themselves.

**Who is at risk (per vendor):**
- Users holding assets **in the App Wallet** → move funds to a hardware wallet or another trusted address **now**.
- Users who **entered a hardware-wallet mnemonic into the app** → that seed is potentially exposed; migrate.
- Users who only **connected a device via the app without ever typing the phrase in** → vendor says no action needed.

The advisory rattled parts of the South Korean crypto community, where DCENT has significant retail presence.

**Root cause:** **Not disclosed.** The company said it would publish technical detail once the investigation confirms the cause and extent. Do not assume seed-extraction, supply-chain compromise, or backend breach — none of those is confirmed.

**Bug class:** key-management (unclassified pending disclosure)

### Flamincome / FlamingoFinance — Flash-Loan Share-Price Inflation (Day 2)

**Chain:** Ethereum
**Status:** Exploit completed **Sept 16**; affected contracts are **legacy Flamincome deployments**, not current FlamingoFinance infrastructure. No official protocol statement has been issued.
**Sept 17 development:** a community PoC landed — DeFiHackLabs PR #1266 — giving the first concrete account of the accounting step Blockaid's alert left unspecified, and the attacker's funding and laundering trail was traced.
**Lost:** ~**$345,900 USDT**.

**Recap:** Blockaid's exploit-detection system flagged an attack on older Flamincome contracts on Sept 16. The attacker took an **~$18M USDT flash loan**, staked USDP LP tokens into a strategy contract, which **raised the reported share price of `VaultYUSDT`**, then redeemed liquid aUSDT at the inflated rate for ~$345.9K profit.

**Contracts referenced:** `0x0461eEFF7C856020E574c0c364FE968Ca06BCc0F`, `0xb8d6471cA573C92c7096Ab8600347F6a9Fe268a5`
**Funding:** linked address `0x83381e7F7232775735169d72D237B858fFc36871` received 0.1 ETH from Tornado Cash roughly two hours before the exploit; proceeds later moved through 144.15 ETH and LI.FI.

**Root cause:** Unsafe share-price / NAV accounting — a permissionless stake into the strategy inflates reported vault NAV, and withdrawal redeems against that inflated NAV in the same transaction. Blockaid's public report **did not specify the precise accounting step** that permitted the inflation. A community PoC (DeFiHackLabs PR #1266) describes it as a permissionless Convex `stakeFor` inflating strategy NAV, with `VaultYUSDT` overpaying on `withdrawAll`.

**Bug class:** flash-loan, price-manipulation (share-price/NAV inflation via permissionless deposit)

### Liquid Network — $320M Cache-Key Collision (Day 12)

**Chain:** Bitcoin (Liquid sidechain, Blockstream federation)
**Status:** Block production **resumed Sept 10**; **peg-outs remain suspended as of Sept 17**. Peg-in via SideSwap reopened around Sept 11. L-BTC still cannot be redeemed for BTC.
**Cumulative:** ~4,000 BTC (~$320M) of the federation's ~4,200 BTC drained. **3,400 BTC returned** by the actor; **~598.5 BTC (~$47M) still outstanding.**

**Day 12 position:** Blockstream laid out a three-stage recovery — restart blocks, replay valid transactions, then reactivate peg operations. Stage three had **not** been reached in this window. Adam Back publicly stated the L-BTC:BTC peg will be covered 1:1. rekt.news published its Liquid post-mortem on **Sept 16**, placing detailed technical analysis into this window even though the incident itself is older.

**Root cause recap:** On **Sept 6, 2026 at 15:53:10 UTC** (Liquid block 4,050,336), a **cache-key collision** in how Elements caches **range-proof verifications** let an attacker submit invalid transaction data that pointed at a previously-approved cached verification result, bypassing full validation and minting ~4,000 unbacked L-BTC, redeemed for real BTC.

**Why it matters structurally:** every federation member runs the same Elements build, so one bug compromises **all** signers simultaneously. Homogeneous validator software is the single point of failure.

**Attribution:** The actor claimed white-hat status via an on-chain `OP_RETURN` message. Neither Blockstream nor the actor has publicly confirmed whether the ~598.5 BTC retained constitutes a de facto bounty. **Motive is self-asserted and unverified.**

**Bug class:** logic-error (validation-cache key collision)

### Nomic → Osmosis allBTC — Forwarding-Logic Double-Spend (Day 9 since disclosure)

**Chain:** Cosmos (Nomic bridge → Osmosis)
**Status:** allBTC minting, redemption, deposits and withdrawals **frozen since Sept 9**; Nomic inflows/outflows frozen; 22.65 BTC locked in the attacker's address by emergency upgrade, awaiting a governance decision. Still frozen through Sept 17.
**Impact:** **39.84 nBTC unbacked — about 36.03% of allBTC's backing**, against 110.57 allBTC in circulation.

**Day 9 position:** No resolution in this window. Full redemption parity needs ~39.84 BTC of valid backing; even if the frozen 22.65 BTC is recovered by governance, roughly **17.19 BTC of additional coverage is still required**.

**Root cause:** A **double-spend flaw in Nomic's custom forwarding logic** allowed false-voucher creation — the same deposit claimed backing more than once. Osmosis confirmed the bug was **upstream in Nomic**, and that neither the Osmosis chain nor IBC itself was compromised.

**The real lesson is detection, not the bug:** principal minting traces to **June 25, 2026**, with further activity **July 17**. It was not discovered or disclosed until **Sept 9–10** — roughly **74 days** during which a wrapped Bitcoin asset traded at par while a third of it was unbacked. Neither project detected it. rekt.news published its Nomic piece Sept 18.

**Bug class:** logic-error (double-spend in bridge forwarding / voucher accounting)

## 4. 🎯 What to hunt

**1. Stop auditing the oracle contract. Audit the oracle's *venue selection*.**

Nostra is the cleanest example this year of an oracle exploit with **zero contract bugs**. The lending logic was fine. The price feed returned exactly what it was asked for. The vulnerability lived in a **third-party aggregator's heuristic for which pool counts as the reference market** — and that heuristic accepted a brand-new, ~1.5-SolvBTC, one-sided pool as canonical.

What to sweep for on any lending market:
- Does the price path terminate at a **first-party on-chain oracle**, or at an aggregator API (GeckoTerminal, DexScreener, CoinGecko) whose pool-selection rules you do not control and cannot audit?
- Can an attacker **create a new pool** for the collateral asset and have it become the reference venue? Is there any minimum-liquidity, minimum-age or whitelist gate on which pools are eligible?
- Is there a **TWAP**, or does the feed read spot? The Nostra pump held for ~2 minutes — a 30-minute TWAP would have absorbed it.

**2. Borrow caps relative to market cap — the missing invariant.**

Nostra let someone borrow **$3.5M against a token with a $546,751 total market cap**. The single highest-value check you can propose to any money market: for every collateral asset, is there a hard cap such that `totalBorrowedAgainst(asset) << fullyDilutedMarketCap(asset)`? Also check for **isolation mode** on long-tail collateral. Governance tokens of the protocol *itself*, accepted as collateral in that same protocol, are the highest-priority target class — the reflexivity is the bug.

**3. Self-referential authorization in `multicall` / batch routers — grep this today.**

The rsETH Safe drain is a pattern you can find statically. Look for any authorization helper that short-circuits on identity:

```
if (_contract == address(this)) return true;
```

…inside a `multicall`, `batch`, `execute`, or `aggregate` entrypoint. That single line converts the router into a **confused deputy**: an attacker calls the router *through itself*, and the nested payload then executes carrying the router's trusted identity. Anything downstream that trusts the router — Safe modules, permission registries, allowlisted spenders — is now reachable.

Concrete sweep:
- Every `multicall(address, bytes[])` where the target address is caller-supplied. Is `address(this)` excluded?
- Every Safe module / permission system that trusts a router contract address. Can that router be induced to call *itself* first?
- Any path where an authorization check and a `DELEGATECALL` sit on the same call chain.

**4. Permissionless deposit → NAV inflation → same-tx withdrawal (Flamincome).**

The classic vault share-price attack is alive and still paying. Check any vault/strategy pair for a function that lets **anyone** increase the strategy's reported assets on behalf of the vault — `stakeFor`, `donate`, `notifyReward`, direct `transfer` into the strategy — without minting corresponding shares. If `pricePerShare = totalAssets / totalSupply` and an attacker can raise `totalAssets` without raising `totalSupply`, then flash-loan → inflate → `withdrawAll` is a one-transaction exploit. Legacy deployments that the team no longer monitors (as here) are where these survive.

**5. Detection gap as a finding in its own right (Nomic).**

The Nomic double-spend ran **74 days** before anyone noticed a third of a wrapped BTC asset was unbacked. For any wrapped/bridged asset, ask: **is there a continuous, automated proof-of-reserves check comparing circulating supply against custodied backing, and does it page someone?** If reconciliation is manual, periodic, or nonexistent, that is reportable — the bug that mints unbacked supply is only half the problem; the other half is that nothing screams. Bridges with **custom forwarding or voucher logic** outside the standard IBC/canonical path are where to concentrate: Osmosis was explicit that IBC itself was fine and the flaw was in Nomic's *custom* forwarding layer.

**6. Federation/validator software homogeneity (Liquid).**

Liquid's cache-key collision compromised every federation signer at once because they all run the same Elements build. For any federated bridge, multisig-of-validators, or permissioned sidechain: is there **any** client diversity, or does one bug in one implementation equal total compromise? Bonus surface — **validation caches**. Any cache keyed on something narrower than the full input it is memoizing (Liquid's range-proof cache) is a collision waiting to be found.
