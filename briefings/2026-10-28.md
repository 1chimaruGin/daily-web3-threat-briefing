### Predict.fun (BNB Chain Prediction Market) — ~$400K Unauthorized Wallet Transfers, Third-Party Documentation Site Blamed

**Chain:** BNB Chain.
**Status:** Active investigation, loss figure confirmed by the company, root cause still preliminary. First flagged ~October 26-27, 2026; company statement October 27, 2026.
**Impact:** ~400,000 USDT (~$400,000) per Predict.fun's own confirmation. An earlier, lower estimate of ~$260,000 (105 ETH) across 19 affected users was reported by on-chain analysts before the company's statement — these two figures describe the same incident at different stages of disclosure, not two separate events.
**What happened:** On-chain analyst "Naeven" first tracked a drain affecting 19 Predict.fun users, estimating roughly 105 ETH (~$260,000) moved out. Predict.fun subsequently posted its own statement confirming that "a small number of users" had experienced unauthorized transfers from their wallets totaling approximately 400,000 USDT, and pledged: "We will fully reimburse all affected users for any funds lost as a direct result of this incident." The company also promised a full post-mortem once its investigation concludes. Stolen funds were reportedly bridged to Ethereum and moved toward Tornado Cash, though this leg of the trace was not independently confirmed in the sources reviewed.
**Root cause:** Preliminary and unconfirmed. Predict.fun's own statement points to its third-party-hosted API documentation site as a possible point of compromise, which the company has since taken offline pending investigation. Critically, no source reviewed explains the technical mechanism by which compromising a documentation site would grant an attacker the ability to move funds out of user wallets — this is the same category of gap seen elsewhere this window (see Bloom, below): a stated "preliminary cause" that has not yet been reconciled with how the actual fund loss occurred.
**Bug class:** infrastructure-compromise / third-party-documentation-site-to-fund-access-gap — the company's named cause (a documentation site) and the observed effect (unauthorized wallet transfers) are not yet connected by any disclosed technical chain.

### Myriad Markets (BNB Chain Prediction Market) — Admin-Resolver Wallet Hijacked via Third-Party Library Flaw, 10 Markets Voided, No Funds Lost

**Chain:** BNB Chain.
**Status:** Contained/resolved. Compromise occurred October 26, 2026; incident report and voiding published October 27, 2026.
**Impact:** $0 realized loss. Myriad says all funds have been recovered and the attacker withdrew nothing; 10 prediction markets are being voided and refunded.
**What happened:** Myriad Markets operates an internal/admin wallet that automatically creates and resolves certain prediction markets. An attacker gained control of this wallet and used it to buy positions in 10 live markets, then abused the wallet's resolver authority to falsely declare those same positions the winning outcome, attempting to claim fraudulent payouts. Affected markets spanned a range of topics, including whether Base would issue a token in 2026, outcomes tied to the US midterm elections, crypto/commodity price levels, and whether George Russell would win the 2026 F1 Drivers' Championship. Myriad says it revoked the compromised wallet's administrative rights within minutes of detecting suspicious activity, and that no attacker ever withdrew funds from the platform. The company is voiding all 10 affected markets and will automatically refund both long and short position holders at each market's last price before the attack, deducting any false profits an attacker (or anyone else) had already claimed; refunds come from recovered funds plus market liquidity pools and require no user action.
**Root cause:** Myriad attributes the admin wallet's key exposure to a vulnerability in an unnamed third-party backend library, stating the exposure itself had existed for "several weeks" before being exploited on October 26 — meaning the window of exposure significantly predates the actual attack.
**Bug class:** access-control / privileged-resolver-wallet-key-exposure-via-third-party-library — a backend dependency flaw leaked credentials for a wallet with centralized, god-mode authority to create and resolve markets, a single point of failure an attacker could use to manufacture winning outcomes rather than needing to touch any on-chain contract logic.

## 4. 🎯 What to hunt

**1. "Preliminary cause" statements that skip the actual fund-movement mechanism (Predict.fun, new this window; Bloom, standing)**

Two incidents in close succession now share the same epistemic gap: a company names a plausible-sounding compromised component (a documentation site for Predict.fun, a third-party module for Bloom) without explaining how that component's compromise translates into the ability to move funds out of user wallets. For hunters, this gap is the interesting part — if a documentation site, SDK, or API gateway is attacker-controlled, check whether it ever serves credentials, signed payloads, embedded code, or links that a user's wallet or a bot's signing flow would trust by default. A stated root cause that doesn't reach all the way to the fund-loss mechanism usually means the real vulnerability is still unpatched.

**2. Centralized "admin"/resolver wallets in prediction markets and similar off-chain-trust systems (Myriad Markets, new this window)**

Myriad's incident is a reminder that prediction markets commonly rely on a single privileged wallet to auto-create and auto-resolve markets — a wallet whose key, once exposed (here, via a vulnerable third-party backend library sitting unnoticed for weeks), lets an attacker manufacture winning outcomes without touching any on-chain logic at all. For any prediction-market, oracle, or outcome-resolution system: map every wallet or key with resolution/settlement authority, check how its key material is stored and what third-party dependencies touch it, and ask what stops a compromised resolver from declaring an attacker-chosen outcome — a question pure smart-contract auditing of the market contracts themselves won't surface.

**3. BNB Chain prediction markets as a newly-concentrated target (Predict.fun + Myriad Markets, both new this window)**

Two distinct BNB Chain prediction-market platforms were hit within roughly 24 hours of each other via two different vectors (a documentation site vs. a backend library). Whether this is coincidence or a sign that prediction markets on BNB Chain are being actively probed as a category is not yet established by available sources — but it's worth treating the prediction-market vertical on BNB Chain as elevated-attention this window, including checking other platforms in the same space for similar third-party-dependency exposure.

**4. Hot-wallet/bot drains that selectively take one asset and leave others (Bloom, standing, new detail)**

Bloom's drain took only SOL and left USDC in the same wallets untouched. For any investigation of a wallet or bot compromise, asset-selectivity is a diagnostic signal: a generic key leak or seed compromise usually sweeps everything reachable, while selective draining suggests either the attacker's tooling was asset-specific (e.g., a SOL-only draining script, or a vulnerability in a SOL-specific signing path) or the access vector itself only covered certain operations. This is worth checking against Bloom's eventual root-cause report when published.

**5. Read the resolution statement, not just the headline figure — still the dominant theme (standing, reinforced)**

This window adds two more examples: Predict.fun's loss figure itself evolved from an analyst's ~$260K estimate to the company's own confirmed ~$400K over about a day, and Bloom's "full reimbursement" claim shipped with an unexplained ~9 SOL shortfall against the amount it said it owed. Treat early loss estimates and company-issued "fully resolved" statements as provisional until the numbers are independently reconciled.
