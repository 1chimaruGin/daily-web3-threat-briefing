> **Scope:** Multi-chain (EVM/Solidity · Solana/Rust · Cosmos/Move · cross-chain bridges). Priority: DeFi, wallets, key infrastructure.
> **Window:** Last ~24–48h (September 29 – October 1 UTC). This is a quiet window by this series' standard: a broad sweep (PeckShield/CertiK/SlowMist alerts, rekt.news, web3isgoinggreat, Cosmos/XRPL/Solana-specific searches, and direct checks on every tracked incident) turned up no new smart-contract or bridge exploit, no new chain-level incident, and no newly disclosed vulnerability that clears this briefing's bar. ## 1. 🔴 Active exploits (last 24h)

No new incident met this briefing's bar for the September 29 – October 1 UTC window. Today's briefing instead covers (a) one piece of new aggregate reporting that puts the prior incidents in context, and (b) incremental updates to the ongoing incidents already being tracked.

### New context: CertiK's September 2026 tally — $766.4M lost, worst month of the year

**Status:** Retrospective monthly report, published **Sept 30**.
**What it says:** CertiK counted **~$766.4M** in crypto losses to exploits and phishing across September 2026 — the highest monthly total and highest incident count of the year, roughly **3.5x** August's $215M. Two incidents — **Bitget (~$387.5M, Sept 24)** and **Liquid Network (~$318.7M, Sept 6)** — made up more than **92%** of the month's confirmed losses. Of the month's total, roughly **$270.6M** was returned or frozen, leaving **~$495.8M** in net unrecovered losses. CertiK's category breakdown: CEX $387.5M, "Mainnet" $325.1M, Individual $18.5M, DeFi $13.3M, Gambling $6.0M (Safe Wallet, DCENT, Duelbits and others each fell in the $3.5-7.8M range).
**Relevance to hunting:** Confirms what this series has already been tracking — the month's losses were dominated by two non-contract-logic incidents (a third-party credential compromise at an exchange, and a client-implementation bug in a Bitcoin sidechain), not by a classic DeFi smart-contract exploit. This continues 2026's broader trend of key/access-control compromises outweighing pure contract-logic bugs in dollar terms.

## 3. 🟢 Advisories & patches

No standalone smart-contract vulnerability disclosure or patch (unrelated to the active/ongoing incidents above) was independently corroborated for the Sept 30 – Oct 1 UTC window. The XRPL BatchV1_1 signature-fix amendment did **not** activate on its earlier Sept 29 target — validator support had briefly dipped below the required 80% threshold, resetting the two-week countdown; as of this window it holds 30-of-35 trusted-validator support (regained Sept 25) and is tracking toward **Oct 9, 14:46 UTC** if that backing holds for the full window. Implementers should still note xrpl.js v5.0.0 produces signatures that BatchV1_1-compliant nodes will reject (v5.1.0 required), and that nodes on client versions before 3.3.0 will become amendment-blocked once it activates.

Separately, CISA and multiple vendors (Rapid7, Tenable, Citrix) disclosed and are tracking active exploitation of two critical Citrix NetScaler ADC/Gateway zero-days — **CVE-2026-88771** and **CVE-2026-88772** (CVSS 9.5, RCE) — with exploitation observed in the wild since Sept 20 and a CISA alert issued Sept 27. This is **general infrastructure security news, not confirmed to be the vector in any tracked crypto incident** — despite surface-level proximity in some aggregator coverage to the Bitget story, no source confirms Bitget's still-unnamed third-party security product is NetScaler, and that connection should not be assumed. Any team running NetScaler ADC/Gateway in front of custody, exchange, or signing infrastructure should patch to 14.1-73.37+ as a precaution regardless.

## 4. 🎯 What to hunt

**1. Chain/network-integration verification for DEXs, bridges and aggregators (DYORSWAP/fake GIWA, Day 4)**

Unchanged guidance: check what a DEX or bridge's actual trust anchor is before it lets users interact with a "new" or "upcoming" chain — chain-ID matching alone is not sufficient verification. DYORSWAP's compensation-plan rollout this window (40% payout under 5 ETH, manual review above) is itself a hunting signal: any protocol that integrates pre-launch networks should have a compensation/kill-switch plan ready *before* this happens, not after.

**2. Privileged "convenience" balance/minting features bolted onto supply-capped chains (Zano Gateway Addresses, Day 5)**

Unchanged: for any L1/L2 adding an "easier integration" code path (batch APIs, account-abstraction shims, custodial-style balances) atop a different underlying accounting model, explicitly test that the new path cannot violate base-model invariants — supply caps, UTXO uniqueness — under adversarial input.

**3. Third-party/vendor components inside the transaction-approval path (Bitget, Day 8, still unresolved)**

Unchanged: map every third-party product with credential-issuing or write access upstream of signing in any custody/multisig/MPC system in scope. Bitget's vendor report is now 6+ days overdue past its "this week" promise — treat sustained non-disclosure of a named vendor as a signal the underlying fix is structurally invasive, not a quick patch.

**4. Reserve-margin fragility after a large incident (Bitget, new this window)**

Bitget's Protection Fund is sitting at only ~3% cushion above its own publicly committed $300M floor after absorbing the $387.5M loss. For any exchange or protocol audit, check whether a stated "fully covered by reserve fund" claim leaves meaningful headroom for a second incident, market-price decline in the fund's BTC-denominated holdings, or continued elevated withdrawal pressure — thin cushions on paper-adequate funds are a real operational risk, not just a PR concern.

**5. Marketplace/UI-level mitigations papering over unpatchable contract bugs (Magic Eden/Limit Break, Day 8)**

Unchanged: Payment Processor V2 remains callable by anyone, forever, on every chain it's deployed to. Any other integrator of the same unpausable contract remains fully exposed regardless of the claim site's existence.

**6. Post-mortem latency as its own signal**

Bitget, Neutron, Payy, Meter.io, Blink, Zano and DYORSWAP are all now carrying promised-but-undelivered technical write-ups, several well over a week overdue (Neutron and Meter.io both now 8+ days past their own stated targets). Don't wait on official post-mortems to reason about root cause from on-chain evidence and third-party analysis.

**7. Governance-cost-to-TVL ratio in Cosmos-SDK/CosmWasm chains (Neutron, Day 9)**

Unchanged: for any CosmWasm protocol in scope, calculate the ratio between the cost of winning a governance vote at spot price right before tally and the value of assets that vote's outcome can control, and check for timelocks between proposal tally and execution.

**8. Client-side signing implementations in hardware/software wallets — treat early figures as a floor (DCENT, Day 17)**

DCENT's XRP leg is now over two weeks old with the signing-compromise mechanism still undisclosed. Prioritize signing/key-derivation code review in any hardware or mobile wallet audit.
