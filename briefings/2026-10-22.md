## 1. 🔴 Active exploits (last 24h)

### NGRAVE — LIQUID App Phishing Attack via Hijacked Push Notifications (~$2.56M, ~70 Wallets)

**Chain:** Multi-chain victim wallets — Bitcoin, XRP Ledger, Ethereum, Stellar.
**Status:** Disclosed by NGRAVE Oct 19, 2026; widely reported Oct 21. Not a smart-contract or hardware exploit — a phishing campaign that abused a trusted notification channel. Attackers compromised a third-party push-notification provider used by NGRAVE's **LIQUID** companion app. Forensic timeline: unauthorized access to the vendor may have begun as early as Oct 11; attackers escalated from an initial access key to a second, more powerful credential with partial access to analytics data. At 21:30 UTC on Oct 17, a fake security alert went out claiming the recipient's wallet had been synced with "a Pixel 9 smartphone located in Moscow." NGRAVE detected the anomaly within about a minute and shut down notifications by 22:45 UTC, but the first wallet was emptied roughly six minutes after the alert went out, and drains continued until 03:51 UTC Oct 18. In total, roughly 70 wallet addresses across four chains lost about **$2.56 million**: ~$1.77M in BTC, ~$454K in XRP, ~$316K in ETH-based assets, and ~$18K in Stellar (XLM).
**What happened / root cause:** Tapping the fraudulent alert opened a spoofed NGRAVE website asking victims to enter their 24-word recovery phrase or their device's "Perfect Key" to "re-verify" their wallet. Entering either credential gave attackers everything needed to reconstruct and drain the wallet. NGRAVE has stated plainly that its **ZERO hardware wallet and private keys were never directly compromised** — every loss traces to a victim entering a secret on a phishing site, not a device-level flaw. NGRAVE's CEO: "In this, we have absolutely and utterly failed," referring to the trust placed in the compromised third-party notification vendor. NGRAVE has since rotated the vendor credentials, added two-person approval for notification sends, restricted sending domains, and added an emergency kill switch; affected users are advised to move funds to a newly created wallet.
**Bug class:** social-engineering / third-party-vendor-impersonation — a supply-chain compromise one layer removed from the wallet itself (the notification pipeline, not the app or hardware), weaponized into an in-brand phishing lure.

## 3. 🟢 Advisories & patches

### PHP2Shell — Two Critical PHP RCEs With Direct Relevance to Exchange/Payment Backends (Oct 21)

**CVE-2026-109618** and **CVE-2026-109619** — together nicknamed "PHP2Shell" — are two critical (CVSS 9.2) remote-code-execution flaws in PHP core, affecting PHP-FPM, Apache mod_php, php-cgi, FrankenPHP, and LiteSpeed, present across **every PHP 7.x and 8.x release**. One analysis suggests at least one of the two is a use-after-free bug; no official root-cause writeup has been published. A proof-of-concept exploit tool leaked before the official fix was ready, forcing the PHP team to move its patch release up by a day; patches landed Oct 21 in PHP 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36, and 8.2.35 (plus minimal backports for 7.4/8.0/8.1). **No workaround exists — patching is the only complete mitigation.** Security researchers (WatchTowr, Huntress, Sansec) observed exploitation attempts both before the fix landed and immediately after, with one firm predicting mass exploitation within minutes of the patch's release. This is not a blockchain-specific bug, but it is a direct threat to crypto infrastructure: any exchange, payment processor, or custodial wallet service running PHP-backed APIs, withdrawal systems, admin panels, or cloud credentials on a vulnerable version is exposed to full remote command execution as the PHP process user — a foothold that, depending on network segmentation, could reach systems adjacent to hot wallets or withdrawal signing.
**Bug class:** memory-safety / use-after-free (infrastructure-level RCE, not a smart-contract bug).

## 4. 🎯 What to hunt

**1. Third-party vendor/notification channels that sit inside a wallet's trust boundary (NGRAVE pattern, new this window)**

NGRAVE's hardware wallet and private keys were never touched — the entire loss came from a compromised *push-notification provider* sending an alert the LIQUID app's own users had been trained to trust. For wallet or wallet-companion-app security work, map every third-party service (notification providers, analytics SDKs, support-chat widgets, update-check endpoints) that can push content a user will see as "from the app," and ask what stops a compromise of that single vendor from becoming an in-brand phishing lure with no code-level exploit required.

**2. PHP/web-infrastructure RCEs as a path into exchange backend systems (PHP2Shell, new this window)**

PHP2Shell is a reminder that a chunk of crypto-exchange and payment-processor attack surface is boring, unpatched web infrastructure rather than Solidity or Rust. If you're doing exchange, custodian, or payment-rail security work, confirm PHP version and patch status on every PHP-backed service in the stack — a single unpatched admin panel or API server is a full RCE away from reaching withdrawal/signing-adjacent systems.

**3. Client-side seed/entropy generation across every wallet-creation surface (Qubic pattern, standing)**

Qubic's compensation progress doesn't change the underlying lesson: the bug sat dormant for over a year because it only affected secondary client surfaces (Safari on Mac, mobile browsers, an old iOS build), not the primary wallet path. Enumerate every platform/browser/legacy-version combination that can generate a seed, not just the flagship client.

**4. Off-chain trust points in bridge mint-authorization pipelines (Cysic pattern, standing — still unresolved)**

Cysic's bridge has now gone five-plus days past the exploit with no published root-cause report. For any bridge audit: enumerate every component — key, relayer, database, API — whose compromise alone is sufficient to trigger a mint/release with no independent on-chain check, and ask what rate limit or multi-party requirement bounds the damage when that single point fails.

**5. Post-mortem latency as its own signal (standing, reinforced this window)**

Gamdom's corrected status is itself an example: a relaunch announcement with compensation bonuses is not a post-mortem, and the attack vector and attacker identity remain unpublished more than a week later. Cysic, Meter.io, DCENT, Fetch.ai, MultiversX, Blink, MALT, and GoldPesa are in the same position. Reason from on-chain evidence and third-party analysis (Blockaid, SlowMist, Bitquery, PeckShield, Arkham, vxdb) rather than waiting for an official writeup that may arrive late, incomplete, or not at all.
